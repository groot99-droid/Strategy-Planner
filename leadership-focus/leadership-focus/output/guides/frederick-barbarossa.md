# Frederick Barbarossa — Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Linear
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Extra district needs population and production to actually fill

> Standard era-block guides work. The ability amplifies a plan rather than supplying one, so the guide's real job is making the player choose a direction the amplifier can act on.

## Ancient / Classical
*Turns: 1 to 80*
- City-state bullying and expansion. Ability not yet leveraged.
- Choose the victory path now. A permanent, unconditional bonus applied to no particular plan produces a comfortable game and no win.
- Respect the one constraint: Extra district needs population and production to actually fill

## Medieval / Renaissance
*Turns: 80 to 160*
- Hansa clusters begin compounding.
- Era checkpoint: confirm the bonus is being spent, not banked. This is the characteristic failure of the shape.

## Industrial / Modern
*Turns: 160 to 240*
- Peak industrial output.
- Era checkpoint: is the chosen victory path still reachable? Linear leaders can pivot; the flat curve makes it affordable.

## Atomic / Information
*Turns: 240 onward*
- Strong production base carries into any victory type.
- Close out the chosen condition.

## Abort branch
**Trigger:** Turn 150 with no victory path committed.
- Pick the path the accumulated empire is already closest to and commit fully. Linear leaders lose by drifting, not by collapsing.

## Fail state
A comfortable, undirected empire that never converts its advantage into a win condition.

## Caveats
- The extra district still requires the population to staff and the production to build it.
- Hansa adjacency depends on Commercial Hubs and resources being placed correctly first.
- Bullying city-states creates lasting grievances and forfeits suzerainty options.

## Guide must account for
- District cap tracked per city, since the extra slot is the whole ability.
- Hansa and Commercial Hub placement planned jointly, before either is built.
- A decision point on bullying versus envoy investment, because they are mutually exclusive.
