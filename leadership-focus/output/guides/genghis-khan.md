# Genghis Khan — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Mongolia  |  **Leader Title:** Khan of Khans
**Source:** Rise and Fall

**Leader Ability — Mongol Horde**
All light and heavy cavalry units, plus the unique Keshig, gain bonus Combat Strength and a chance to capture defeated enemy cavalry units instead of destroying them, letting Genghis Khan's armies grow stronger the more they fight.

**Agenda — Horse Lord**
Genghis Khan strives to build the world's strongest cavalry force and dislikes other civilizations that field powerful cavalry armies of their own.

**Civilization Ability — Örtöö**
Trade Routes to and from Mongolia instantly build a Trading Post in the destination city and grant an extra level of Diplomatic Visibility, echoing the historical Mongol relay-messenger and trade network.

**Unique Units**
- Keshig — A ranged cavalry unit unique to Mongolia that benefits from Genghis Khan's Combat Strength bonus on defense and can capture defeated enemy cavalry, reinforcing the civ's snowballing horde playstyle.

### Historical Background
Genghis Khan founded and became the first Great Khan of the Mongol Empire in the early 13th century, uniting the fractious tribes of the Central Asian steppe and forging, through his descendants, the largest contiguous land empire in world history. His success relied on highly mobile, disciplined cavalry armies, meritocratic promotion, and an extensive relay communication and trade network known as the Yam — the historical basis for Mongolia's Örtöö civilization ability. His campaigns were also marked by devastating psychological warfare and rapid conquest, a legacy echoed in-game by a leader ability that lets Mongol cavalry armies grow stronger by absorbing defeated enemy horsemen.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Gate
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Requires trade route setup before each target

> The strategy does not begin on a turn. It begins on an unlock. This needs two complete guides with a trigger between them, not one guide with a note in the middle.

### Pre-Gate
*Turns: 1 until the gate opens*

Survive, develop, and stage. This is a real phase with its own complete build order, not a waiting room.

- State the gate explicitly on turn 1 so the player knows what they are working toward: Trade route established and running to the intended target before declaring
- Give an estimated arrival turn AND the research or civic path that gets there fastest.
- Build the prerequisites the gate assumes but does not provide. Requires trade route setup before each target
- Maintain a defensive floor. The gate does not protect you while you wait for it.

### Gate Check
*Turns: variable*

A single decision point, not a phase.


### Post-Gate
*Turns: gate onward*

The actual leader strategy, now live.

- Peak. Knights and Keshigs with captured cavalry compounding.
- Run the closing-window check: most Gate abilities are also time-limited on the far end. Identify when parity arrives.
- Convert before parity. Permanent

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Gate opens with staging incomplete and a rival is already at parity.
- Do not open the strategy. Playing an unstaged Gate leader into a prepared opponent is the worst outcome available.
- Fold into a generic plan built on whatever the pre-gate phase produced.

### Fail state
The gate opened, the window closed, and the strategy never ran. The pre-gate phase was treated as filler and produced nothing usable.

### Caveats
- The trade route must reach and survive at the target, which means setting it up well before hostility.
- Capturing defeated cavalry only pays against civs that actually field cavalry.
- Horse resource access is a hard prerequisite the ability does not provide.

### Guide must account for
- Trade route dispatch as an explicit pre-war step with its own lead time.
- A horse and strategic resource audit before committing to the plan.
- Target selection filtered by whether the enemy fields capturable units.
