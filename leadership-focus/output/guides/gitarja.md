# Gitarja — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Indonesia  |  **Leader Title:** none
**Source:** Base Game

**Leader Ability — Exalted Goddess of the Three Worlds**
Gitarja can purchase naval units with Faith and her religious units can embark and disembark for free, while City Centers adjacent to Coast or Lake tiles gain bonus Faith, tying Indonesia's naval and religious games together.

**Agenda — Archipelagic State**
Gitarja favors civilizations that avoid settling or conquering cities on small, isolated landmasses and dislikes those with many such cities.

**Civilization Ability — Great Nusantara**
Coast and Lake tiles grant a minor adjacency bonus to Holy Sites, Campuses, Industrial Zones, and Theater Squares, and Entertainment Complexes gain extra Amenities, making coastal district placement especially rewarding for Indonesia.

**Unique Units**
- Jong (replaces Frigate) — A unique naval ranged unit with higher movement than the Frigate it replaces, giving Indonesia strong early control of the seas.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Kampung (Improvement) — A coastal tile improvement built adjacent to a water resource that grants Housing, Production, and Food scaling with adjacent Fishing Boats, supporting dense coastal cities.

### Historical Background
Gitarja (Dyah Gitarja, also known as Tribhuwana Wijayatunggadewi, reigned 1328-1350) was a queen of the Majapahit Empire, one of the last and largest Hindu-Buddhist thalassocratic empires of maritime Southeast Asia, centered on Java. Under her rule and that of her son Hayam Wuruk, Majapahit extended influence across a vast archipelago through naval power and control of inter-island trade routes rather than contiguous land conquest. This archipelago-spanning, sea-based model of statecraft is the basis for Indonesia's in-game focus on coastal adjacency bonuses, naval units, and an agenda that rewards leaving small islands to Indonesia's control.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Gate
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Water-heavy map required; dead on continents

> The strategy does not begin on a turn. It begins on an unlock. This needs two complete guides with a trigger between them, not one guide with a note in the middle.

### Pre-Gate
*Turns: 1 until the gate opens*

Survive, develop, and stage. This is a real phase with its own complete build order, not a waiting room.

- State the gate explicitly on turn 1 so the player knows what they are working toward: Coastal Holy Sites generating enough Faith to fund a Jong purchase, on a water-heavy map
- Give an estimated arrival turn AND the research or civic path that gets there fastest.
- Build the prerequisites the gate assumes but does not provide. Water-heavy map required; dead on continents
- Maintain a defensive floor. The gate does not protect you while you wait for it.

### Gate Check
*Turns: variable*

A single decision point, not a phase.


### Post-Gate
*Turns: gate onward*

The actual leader strategy, now live.

- Peak. Faith-purchased Jong armadas.
- Run the closing-window check: most Gate abilities are also time-limited on the far end. Identify when parity arrives.
- Convert before parity. Permanent

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Gate opens with staging incomplete and a rival is already at parity.
- Do not open the strategy. Playing an unstaged Gate leader into a prepared opponent is the worst outcome available.
- Fold into a generic plan built on whatever the pre-gate phase produced.

### Fail state
The gate opened, the window closed, and the strategy never ran. The pre-gate phase was treated as filler and produced nothing usable.

### Caveats
- Continental maps disable the strategy almost completely.
- Coastal cities are production-poor, which is why Faith purchasing matters, but it also caps everything else.
- The Jong window closes; Faith spent too late buys obsolete ships.

### Guide must account for
- A map viability check before the strategy is recommended at all.
- Navy purchase timing set against the obsolescence window, not against Faith availability.
- A land plan, since coastal cities alone rarely carry a full game.
