# Ludwig II — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Germany  |  **Leader Title:** King of Bavaria
**Source:** New Frontier Pass / Leader Pass (Great Builders Pack persona)

**Leader Ability — Swan King**
All Wonders (finished or still under construction) grant 2 Culture from each adjacent District, and after researching Castles, all Culture adjacency bonuses also generate Tourism — turning a wonder-dense empire into a strong late-game Culture/Tourism engine.

**Agenda — Eternal Enigma**
Ludwig favors civilizations that do not max out the number of Districts in their cities, and dislikes those who pack their cities with as many Districts as possible.

**Civilization Ability — Free Imperial Cities**
German cities can build one additional District beyond what their Population would normally allow, letting Germany build wider, more specialized cities earlier than other civilizations.

**Unique Units**
- U-Boat (replaces Submarine) — A stronger, stealthier submarine unit reflecting Germany's historical submarine warfare, useful for both raiding and area denial at sea.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Hansa (District) — Germany's unique Industrial Zone replacement: cheaper to build, with better adjacency bonuses (from Commercial Hubs and resources) for Production, making it easier to power through Ludwig's many Wonders.

### Historical Background
Ludwig II (1845-1886) was King of Bavaria, famous less for statecraft than for his extravagant, fairy-tale castle-building projects — most notably Neuschwanstein Castle — and his devoted patronage of composer Richard Wagner, which together earned him the nickname 'the Swan King,' the name of his in-game ability. His reign saw Bavaria's political independence curtailed as Germany unified under Prussian leadership, and he died in disputed circumstances shortly after being declared mentally unfit to rule. As a Leader Pass persona representing Germany despite historically ruling only Bavaria, his in-game focus on wonder-building and culture/tourism is a direct nod to his architectural obsessions.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Monitor
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Conditional | **Condition cost:** Requires deliberately leaving Wonders unfinished, locking up production

> The core loop is a stat check, not a build order. These guides fail hardest when written as sequences, because the player's real job is watching one number and responding to it.

### Setup
*Turns: 1 to roughly 25*

Establish the thing being monitored and the infrastructure that lets you respond to it.

- Name the watched stat on turn 1: Rival production progress on each stalled Wonder
- Establish its bands before it matters, so the player recognizes trouble the first time rather than the third.
- Build the response capacity. A monitor with no available response is just anxiety.

### Standing Loop
*Turns: 25 to end of game*

This replaces the turn list entirely. The guide is this loop plus the era checkpoints.

- Read Rival production progress on each stalled Wonder.
- Green band: continue current build. No action.
- Amber band: begin the correction now. Amber is the acting band; red is the failing band.
- Red band: the ability is off or about to be. Everything else is deprioritized until it clears.

### Era Checkpoints
*Turns: each era boundary*

Re-read the role. Monitor leaders change what the stat means across eras.

- Ancient/Classical: Nothing meaningful.
- Medieval/Renaissance: Peak. Half-built Wonders as a culture engine.
- Industrial/Modern: Culture converting to tourism.
- Atomic/Information: Culture victory position.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Watched stat sits in the red band for more than roughly 15 turns with no recovery path.
- Stop paying the Requires deliberately leaving Wonders unfinished, locking up production. If the bonus is unreachable, its price is pure loss.
- Rebuild as a generic civ around whatever the monitored strategy already produced.

### Fail state
The stat crossed into red and stayed. For Ludwig II, Completing the Wonder ends the bonus. This is the only self-terminating ability on the roster.

### Caveats
- Completing the Wonder ends the bonus. This is the only self-terminating ability on the roster.
- A rival completing that Wonder destroys the engine and wastes all invested production.
- Production locked in permanently unfinished builds is production doing nothing else.

### Guide must account for
- A designated stall list: which Wonders are deliberately never finished.
- Rival wonder progress as a monitored risk, with a finish-it-now trigger.
- A rule for when completing is better than stalling, since it changes late in the game.
