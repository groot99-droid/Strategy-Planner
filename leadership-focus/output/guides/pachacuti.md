# Pachacuti — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Incan  |  **Leader Title:** Sapa Inca ("the Earth Shaker")
**Source:** Gathering Storm

**Leader Ability — Qhapaq Ñan**
Domestic Trade Routes gain bonus Food for every Mountain tile near their origin city, and the Incas gain access to the Qhapaq Ñan unique tile improvement once the Foreign Trade civic is researched, rewarding mountain-heavy empires with strong internal trade.

**Agenda — Sapa Inca**
Pachacuti wants to settle his own cities near Mountains and is happy to let other civilizations avoid that terrain, but dislikes rival leaders who also settle near Mountains and compete with him for that land.

**Civilization Ability — Mit'a**
Incan citizens can work Mountain tiles directly for Production (with an increased yield reached in the Industrial Era), and Mountains provide bonus Food to adjacent Terrace Farms, letting the Inca build thriving cities in terrain other civilizations must avoid.

**Unique Units**
- Warak'aq (replaces Skirmisher) — A ranged unit with notably higher Ranged Strength than the Skirmisher it replaces, giving the Inca strong early ranged offense to complement their mountain-based defense.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Terrace Farm (Improvement) — A unique farm improvement buildable on hills/grassland/plains/desert that gets bonus Food next to Mountains and bonus Production next to an Aqueduct, letting mountain cities also feed themselves.
- Qhapaq Ñan (Improvement) — A unique road-like mountain-tunnel improvement unlocked by Pachacuti's leader ability, granted automatically with Foreign Trade, that helps connect an Inca empire fragmented by mountainous terrain.

### Historical Background
Pachacuti Inca Yupanqui ruled from roughly 1438 to 1471 and is credited with transforming the small kingdom of Cusco into the vast Inca Empire (Tawantinsuyu) through a combination of military conquest and sweeping administrative reform. He is traditionally credited with commissioning Machu Picchu and with building out the empire's remarkable road network, the Qhapaq Ñan, which linked its Andean territories across extremely difficult mountain terrain -- the direct namesake of his in-game leader ability and unique improvement. His empire's mastery of mountain agriculture, especially terrace farming, let the Inca feed large populations in terrain most other pre-modern states avoided, which is exactly what his in-game civilization ability rewards.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Front-Load
**Curve:** Flat | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Mountain terrain dependency; dead start without it

> Nearly every decision that matters is made in the first twenty turns and cannot be reversed. Guide density should be inverted: dense at the start, sparse after.

### Turn 0 — Assessment
*Turns: before the first Settler moves*

A genuine go or no-go on the start position. This phase exists only in this shape and it is the most valuable thing in the template.

- Check the hard requirement: Mountain terrain dependency; dead start without it
- If the requirement is absent within a reasonable walk, decide now whether to move the Settler or play the leader as a generic civ. Deciding this at turn 40 is deciding it too late.
- Estimate the ceiling. For radius- and continent-capped leaders, the ceiling is knowable early and determines the victory path.

### Turns 1-20 — Commit
*Turns: 1 to 20*

Make every irreversible decision deliberately rather than incidentally.

- Draw the full settle map before the second Settler is produced, including sites you will not reach for 40 turns.
- Assign district roles to each city site at settle time, not at build time. For placement-restricted leaders this is the whole game.
- Mark each decision with its reversibility. The guide should say plainly which choices are permanent.
- A start assessment step with a genuine go or no-go on the position.

### Turns 20-80 — Execute
*Turns: 20 to 80*

Build out the plan drawn in the commit phase. Low decision density by design.

- Follow the settle map. Deviation is the common failure, and it usually feels reasonable at the time.
- High-population fortified cities coming online.

### Turn 80+ — Live With It
*Turns: 80 onward*

Operate inside the ceiling set at turn 20.

- Choose a victory path that fits the ceiling rather than fighting it.
- Strong tall empire with natural defense.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Turn 0 assessment fails and no acceptable alternative start is within reach.
- Play the leader as a generic civ and say so explicitly. A named fallback is better than a strategy that quietly cannot work.
- Target a faster victory, since the capped ceiling will not win a long game.

### Fail state
A placement decision made before turn 20 has capped the game, and the cap will not be visible until roughly turn 100. A start without mountains makes the leader ordinary at best. Start roll variance is the highest on the roster.

### Caveats
- A start without mountains makes the leader ordinary at best. Start roll variance is the highest on the roster.
- Mountains block your own unit movement as effectively as they block invaders.
- Early production from mountain-heavy terrain is low even with the ability.

### Guide must account for
- A start assessment step with a genuine go or no-go on the position.
- Terrace Farm placement planned against mountain adjacency from the first Builder.
- An early production supplement plan, since the terrain does not provide one.
