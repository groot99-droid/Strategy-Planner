# Gorgo — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Greece  |  **Leader Title:** Warrior Queen of Sparta
**Source:** Base Game

**Leader Ability — Thermopylae**
Every combat victory grants Culture equal to a portion of the defeated unit's Combat Strength, turning Gorgo's wars into a steady source of Culture generation alongside conquest.

**Agenda — With Your Shield Or On It**
Gorgo never yields territory or cities in a peace deal and admires leaders who likewise refuse to concede in negotiations; she dislikes anyone who has surrendered in a peace deal or who has never gone to war at all. In Gathering Storm, Grievances against her decay at twice the normal rate.

**Civilization Ability — Plato's Republic**
Greece gains an extra Wildcard policy slot in every government, giving it unmatched flexibility to mix and match policy cards regardless of which government is in use.

**Unique Units**
- Hoplite (replaces Spearman) — Gains a large Combat Strength bonus when adjacent to another Hoplite, rewarding tightly packed phalanx-style formations reminiscent of Greek warfare.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Acropolis (District) — A unique district (best placed on hills) that generates strong Culture output, reinforcing Greece's natural strength in a Culture Victory.

### Historical Background
Gorgo was queen of Sparta and wife of King Leonidas I, one of the few women of the ancient Greek world whose political wisdom and outspokenness were specifically recorded by the historian Herodotus. Leonidas famously led 300 Spartans (with allied Greek forces) in a last stand against the invading Persian army at the Battle of Thermopylae in 480 BCE, an event directly named in Gorgo's leader ability. Spartan culture prized unyielding martial resolve, epitomized by the (possibly apocryphal) saying attributed to Spartan mothers urging sons to return from battle 'with your shield, or on it' — the namesake of Gorgo's agenda, which rewards refusing to back down in war or negotiation.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Monitor
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Requires a continuous supply of things to kill

> The core loop is a stat check, not a build order. These guides fail hardest when written as sequences, because the player's real job is watching one number and responding to it.

### Setup
*Turns: 1 to roughly 25*

Establish the thing being monitored and the infrastructure that lets you respond to it.

- Name the watched stat on turn 1: Available kill sources (barbarian camp count, active wars)
- Establish its bands before it matters, so the player recognizes trouble the first time rather than the third.
- Build the response capacity. A monitor with no available response is just anxiety.

### Standing Loop
*Turns: 25 to end of game*

This replaces the turn list entirely. The guide is this loop plus the era checkpoints.

- Read Available kill sources (barbarian camp count, active wars).
- Green band: continue current build. No action.
- Amber band: begin the correction now. Amber is the acting band; red is the failing band.
- Red band: the ability is off or about to be. Everything else is deprioritized until it clears.

### Era Checkpoints
*Turns: each era boundary*

Re-read the role. Monitor leaders change what the stat means across eras.

- Ancient/Classical: Barbarian farming into an early civic lead.
- Medieval/Renaissance: Culture lead cashed into governments and policy cards ahead of schedule.
- Industrial/Modern: Combat culture diluted; the lead must already exist.
- Atomic/Information: Irrelevant.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Watched stat sits in the red band for more than roughly 15 turns with no recovery path.
- Stop paying the Requires a continuous supply of things to kill. If the bonus is unreachable, its price is pure loss.
- Rebuild as a generic civ around whatever the monitored strategy already produced.

### Fail state
The stat crossed into red and stayed. For Gorgo, Culture comes from killing units, not from capturing cities. A clean city grab pays nothing.

### Caveats
- Culture comes from killing units, not from capturing cities. A clean city grab pays nothing.
- Barbarian supply dries up as the map is settled, and that is usually before the ability stops mattering.
- The culture is only worth what you unlock with it; hoarding it is the common failure.

### Guide must account for
- A kill-source pipeline as a maintained resource, with a plan for when barbarians run out.
- A pre-committed civic target list so the culture spike converts immediately.
- A warning that siege-heavy, low-kill warfare produces almost nothing for her.
