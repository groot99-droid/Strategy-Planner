# Seondeok — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Korean  |  **Leader Title:** Queen of Silla
**Source:** Rise and Fall

**Leader Ability — Hwarang**
Each promotion a Governor has earned grants a percentage boost to both Culture and Science in the city they're assigned to, meaning heavily-promoted Governors turn into powerful Science and Culture engines under Seondeok.

**Agenda — Cheomseongdae**
Seondeok focuses on maximizing her own empire's Science output and dislikes civilizations that produce comparatively little Science.

**Civilization Ability — Three Kingdoms**
Mines gain a Science yield and Farms gain a Food yield when placed adjacent to Korea's unique Seowon district, letting Korea weave its normal tile improvements directly into a Science-focused district strategy.

**Unique Units**
- Hwacha (replaces Field Cannon) — A powerful Renaissance-era ranged siege unit that cannot move and attack in the same turn, trading mobility for strong ranged damage output.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Seowon (District) — Replaces the Campus and is Korea's central Science district, gaining adjacency bonuses from Mines and Farms placed near it rather than from the usual mountains/rainforest, tying directly into the Three Kingdoms civilization ability.

### Historical Background
Seondeok reigned as Queen of Silla, one of Korea's Three Kingdoms, from 632 to 647 CE, becoming the first reigning queen in Korean history. Her rule is remembered for its patronage of Buddhism, learning, and science, including the construction of Cheomseongdae, widely regarded as the oldest surviving astronomical observatory in East Asia and the direct namesake of her in-game agenda. She also strengthened Silla's diplomatic ties with Tang China during a period of intense rivalry among Korea's Three Kingdoms, groundwork that helped Silla eventually unify the peninsula after her reign. Her hallmark blend of scholarly patronage, astronomy, and governance is reflected in her in-game kit's emphasis on Science output and Governor-driven yield bonuses.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Front-Load
**Curve:** Flat | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Seowon isolation forfeits all district adjacency stacking

> Nearly every decision that matters is made in the first twenty turns and cannot be reversed. Guide density should be inverted: dense at the start, sparse after.

### Turn 0 — Assessment
*Turns: before the first Settler moves*

A genuine go or no-go on the start position. This phase exists only in this shape and it is the most valuable thing in the template.

- Check the hard requirement: Seowon isolation forfeits all district adjacency stacking
- If the requirement is absent within a reasonable walk, decide now whether to move the Settler or play the leader as a generic civ. Deciding this at turn 40 is deciding it too late.
- Estimate the ceiling. For radius- and continent-capped leaders, the ceiling is knowable early and determines the victory path.

### Turns 1-20 — Commit
*Turns: 1 to 20*

Make every irreversible decision deliberately rather than incidentally.

- Draw the full settle map before the second Settler is produced, including sites you will not reach for 40 turns.
- Assign district roles to each city site at settle time, not at build time. For placement-restricted leaders this is the whole game.
- Mark each decision with its reversibility. The guide should say plainly which choices are permanent.
- A hill inventory taken at settle time, since Seowon placement is decided then.

### Turns 20-80 — Execute
*Turns: 20 to 80*

Build out the plan drawn in the commit phase. Low decision density by design.

- Follow the settle map. Deviation is the common failure, and it usually feels reasonable at the time.
- Steady, unspectacular science lead.

### Turn 80+ — Live With It
*Turns: 80 onward*

Operate inside the ceiling set at turn 20.

- Choose a victory path that fits the ceiling rather than fighting it.
- Ceiling shows. Rivals with real adjacency overtake.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Turn 0 assessment fails and no acceptable alternative start is within reach.
- Play the leader as a generic civ and say so explicitly. A named fallback is better than a strategy that quietly cannot work.
- Target a faster victory, since the capped ceiling will not win a long game.

### Fail state
A placement decision made before turn 20 has capped the game, and the cap will not be visible until roughly turn 100. Isolation forfeits all district adjacency stacking, so the science ceiling is genuinely low.

### Caveats
- Isolation forfeits all district adjacency stacking, so the science ceiling is genuinely low.
- Hills are a hard requirement for the full bonus and constrain city siting.
- Verify the isolated adjacency figure before publishing it; sources cite both +3 and +4.

### Guide must account for
- A hill inventory taken at settle time, since Seowon placement is decided then.
- District spacing rules that keep Seowons isolated, which conflicts with normal placement instincts.
- A faster victory timeline, because the ceiling does not reward a long game.
