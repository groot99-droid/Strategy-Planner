# Basil II — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Byzantium  |  **Leader Title:** none
**Source:** New Frontier Pass

**Leader Ability — Porphyrogénnētos**
Basil's heavy and light cavalry units deal full damage when attacking cities that follow the same religion as Byzantium, and researching Divine Right unlocks his powerful unique Tagma unit, rewarding a combined religious-military conquest strategy.

**Agenda — Divine Guardian**
Basil II focuses on spreading his religion and defending it, favoring civilizations that share his faith and disliking those that follow a different one.

**Civilization Ability — Taxis**
Byzantine units gain bonus Combat and Religious Strength for every Holy City following Byzantium's religion (including its own), Byzantium's religion spreads automatically when an enemy unit is defeated in combat, and Holy Site cities earn extra Great Prophet points.

**Unique Units**
- Tagma (replaces Knight) — Unlocked via Basil's leader ability upon researching Divine Right; it grants Combat or Religious Strength to nearby friendly land units, functioning as a mobile support unit as well as a strong attacker.
- Dromon (replaces Quadrireme) — A naval melee unit with greater range and combat strength than the Quadrireme, giving Byzantium an early naval edge.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Hippodrome (District) — Replaces the Entertainment Complex, costs less Production, grants extra Amenities, and spawns a free Heavy Cavalry unit each time the district or one of its buildings is completed.

### Historical Background
Basil II (reigned 976-1025) was one of the Byzantine Empire's most formidable emperors, expanding its borders to their greatest extent since Justinian through decades of relentless campaigning, most famously his prolonged conquest of the First Bulgarian Empire, which earned him the epithet 'Bulgar-Slayer.' Born 'porphyrogennetos' ('born in the purple'), meaning born to a reigning emperor in the palace's porphyry chamber, he ruled as a soldier-emperor who tied military success closely to Orthodox Christian identity and left the treasury and empire stronger than he found them. His fusion of religious authority with battlefield conquest is captured directly in his leader ability's name and its focus on converting military victories into religious dominance.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Gate
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Requires founding a religion AND spreading it to the target first

> The strategy does not begin on a turn. It begins on an unlock. This needs two complete guides with a trigger between them, not one guide with a note in the middle.

### Pre-Gate
*Turns: 1 until the gate opens*

Survive, develop, and stage. This is a real phase with its own complete build order, not a waiting room.

- State the gate explicitly on turn 1 so the player knows what they are working toward: Religion founded AND that religion spread to the intended target city
- Give an estimated arrival turn AND the research or civic path that gets there fastest.
- Build the prerequisites the gate assumes but does not provide. Requires founding a religion AND spreading it to the target first
- Maintain a defensive floor. The gate does not protect you while you wait for it.

### Gate Check
*Turns: variable*

A single decision point, not a phase.


### Post-Gate
*Turns: gate onward*

The actual leader strategy, now live.

- Peak. Cavalry ignoring walls against converted cities.
- Run the closing-window check: most Gate abilities are also time-limited on the far end. Identify when parity arrives.
- Convert before parity. Permanent

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Gate opens with staging incomplete and a rival is already at parity.
- Do not open the strategy. Playing an unstaged Gate leader into a prepared opponent is the worst outcome available.
- Fold into a generic plan built on whatever the pre-gate phase produced.

### Fail state
The gate opened, the window closed, and the strategy never ran. The pre-gate phase was treated as filler and produced nothing usable.

### Caveats
- Two prerequisites must both be met before the bonus exists: found the religion, then spread it to the target.
- Missionaries and Apostles must survive the trip, and they are fragile.
- If the target converts away, the bonus disappears mid-campaign.

### Guide must account for
- Religious spread treated as a military logistics step with its own timeline.
- Escort planning for religious units moving toward hostile territory.
- Target faith status as a per-turn check during the war, not just before it.
