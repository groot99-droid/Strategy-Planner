# Suleiman — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Ottoman  |  **Leader Title:** Suleiman I, The Magnificent (also known as Kanuni, "The Lawgiver")
**Source:** Base Game

**Leader Ability — Grand Vizier**
Suleiman gains access to a unique, exclusive Governor (Ibrahim) with strong military and diplomatic bonuses, and unlocks the Janissary unique unit plus a bonus Governor Title once Gunpowder is researched. It lets the Ottomans field an elite late-game army while also running a top-tier governor for loyalty and diplomacy.

**Agenda — Lawgiver**
Suleiman keeps his own cities happy and loyal, and is impressed by civilizations that do the same or have founded many cities; he dislikes leaders whose cities are unhappy, disloyal, or few in number due to conquest.

**Civilization Ability — Great Turkish Bombard**
Ottoman siege units are trained 50% faster and gain +5 Combat Strength when attacking or defending against district defenses, making the Ottomans exceptionally strong at both offensive sieges and city-state coercion.

**Unique Units**
- Janissary (replaces Musketman) — Cheaper and stronger than the Musketman, starts with a free promotion, and can convert a defeated enemy unit into an Ottoman unit, letting Ottoman armies grow while attacking.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Grand Bazaar (Building) — Replaces the Bank in the Commercial Hub, is the cheapest unique building in the game, and adds bonus Amenities and access to extra strategic resources.

### Historical Background
Suleiman I (r. 1520-1566) presided over the Ottoman Empire at its territorial and cultural zenith, pushing its borders deep into Hungary and the Middle East while his fleets contested the Mediterranean. Ottomans called him 'Kanuni' (the Lawgiver) for his sweeping legal and administrative reforms that brought consistency to a sprawling multiethnic empire, while Europeans knew him as 'the Magnificent' for his court's wealth and patronage of architecture (including the Süleymaniye Mosque) and the arts. His reign relied heavily on the elite, gunpowder-armed Janissary corps and an efficient siege-focused military, both reflected in the Ottomans' in-game strengths. This mix of centralizing governance and relentless military expansion is the throughline connecting the historical sultan to his in-game kit of governance-boosting abilities and siege-crushing bombards.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Dead-Phase
**Curve:** Bloom | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Nothing early, which is also the problem

> The hardest template to write and the most necessary. The leader contributes nothing for 80 to 120 turns. Without an explicit early game AND an explicit statement that the flatness is intended, players abandon the plan around turn 60.

### Phase A — Dormant
*Turns: 1 to roughly 110*

Survive, develop, and quietly accumulate the prerequisite the payoff phase consumes.

- Run a complete generic build order. The guide must supply this in full, because the leader supplies no direction.
- Maintain a defensive floor. Elimination during Phase A is the single most common way these leaders lose, and it is invisible in the ability description.
- Accumulate the prerequisite even though it produces nothing yet: Siege-capable production base and the unique governor already placed in position
- Provide non-ability progress markers so the player has feedback: city count, science rate, district count. Something must be visibly improving.

### Phase B — Activation
*Turns: roughly 110*

The kit switches on. Verify the accumulated prerequisite is actually sufficient.

- Check the stockpile. Activation with an empty stockpile wastes the entire dormant phase.
- Peak. Siege specialization against fortified capitals.
- Reorient production toward the payoff. Phase A build habits are now wrong.

### Phase C — Payoff
*Turns: 110 onward*

Convert the accumulated position at speed. The remaining game is short, so this phase runs hot.

- Still functional.
- Push the victory condition directly. There is no time for a second build-out.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Reaching activation with the prerequisite stockpile empty, or having lost cities during the dormant phase.
- Accept a lesser victory path built on the generic empire rather than the leader ability.
- The leader ability is now decorative. Say so and stop planning around it.

### Fail state
Crippled or eliminated before activation, or arriving at activation with nothing stockpiled. Both are Phase A failures that only become visible in Phase B.

### Caveats
- There is no early identity at all. The guide has to fill roughly 100 turns with generic play.
- Janissary recruitment costs population, which competes directly with growth.
- The unique governor needs establishment time in place before it affects anything.

### Guide must account for
- A complete generic early build order, because the leader supplies no direction there.
- Governor placement scheduled well ahead of the intended war, not at declaration.
- A defensive floor for the long dormant phase, since the kit offers no early protection.
