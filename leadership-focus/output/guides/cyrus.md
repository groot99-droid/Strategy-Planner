# Cyrus — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Persia  |  **Leader Title:** The Great
**Source:** Base Game

**Leader Ability — Fall of Babylon**
For a number of turns after declaring a Surprise War, Cyrus's units gain bonus Movement and other yields, and cities he occupies with a garrisoned unit receive extra Loyalty, softening the usual drawbacks of launching a sudden attack.

**Agenda — Opportunist**
Cyrus favors leaders who have themselves declared Surprise Wars and dislikes those who have never done so.

**Civilization Ability — Satrapies**
Persia gains a free Trade Route upon researching Political Philosophy, and its internal (domestic) Trade Routes generate enhanced yields, rewarding a strong internal trade network alongside military expansion.

**Unique Units**
- Immortal (replaces Swordsman) — Gains a bonus ranged attack and strong defense, making it unusually versatile compared to a standard melee Swordsman.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Pairidaeza (Improvement) — A unique tile improvement ('paradise garden') that boosts Culture, Gold, and Appeal, with further bonuses based on adjacent districts, supporting a strong domestic economy.

### Historical Background
Cyrus II, known as Cyrus the Great, founded the Achaemenid Persian Empire in the mid-6th century BCE, rapidly conquering the Median, Lydian, and Neo-Babylonian empires (including the fall of Babylon referenced in his leader ability) to create the largest empire the world had yet seen. He was noted for relatively tolerant governance of conquered peoples, exemplified by the Cyrus Cylinder, and for a career built on swift, decisive military campaigns — reflected in his ability's rewards for surprise warfare. Cyrus was ultimately killed in 530 BCE in battle against Tomyris and the Massagetae, a fitting rival matchup within Civilization VI given her leader ability is literally named for his death.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Countdown
**Curve:** Spike | **Conversion:** Expiring | **Condition cost:** Surprise War diplomatic and grievance penalties
**Convert by:** turn 90

> The ability has an expiry. The guide is a timer wrapped around a conversion deadline. Its most important content is not the build order, it is the turn the plan stops and what replaces it.

### Phase 1 — Arm
*Turns: 1 to roughly 30*

Reach the window with everything already staged. Nothing in this phase is optional; staging done after the window opens is spent from the budget.

- Turn 1: identify the target. Cyrus's window is short enough that target selection cannot wait for scouting to finish.
- Turns 1-15: scout toward the target only. Exploration away from the target is a luxury this shape cannot afford.
- Turns 10-30: production goes to the units or charges the window will consume. Infrastructure that pays back after 90 is deferred.
- Pre-window checkpoint: confirm Surprise War diplomatic and grievance penalties is satisfied and units are within striking distance, not at home.

### Phase 2 — Window
*Turns: roughly 30 to 90*

Convert tempo into permanent assets. Cities, wonders, or improvements. Not kills, not gold, not map presence.


**Checkpoints:**
- *midpoint of window*: Is at least half the objective secured? → if yes: proceed; if no: Reduce scope now. Take one city instead of three. Partial conversion beats total expiry.
- *90*: Has the tempo become something permanent? → if yes: Proceed to Phase 3.; if no: Abort branch.

### Phase 3 — Digest
*Turns: 90 onward*

Stabilize what was taken. This phase is longer than both prior phases combined and most guides for this shape underwrite it badly.

- Amenity and loyalty audit on every acquired city before any new construction.
- Infrastructure catch-up: the arming phase deliberately skipped this, and the bill comes due now.
- Choose a victory path. Cyrus has no leader-specific direction from here; Ten turns no longer crosses the distances involved.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Window closed with no permanent gain, or three stalled turns before the midpoint.
- Make peace immediately. Prolonging a failed window converts a bad position into a lost game.
- Disband surplus units. The maintenance is now pure loss.
- Switch to the generic development plan for the empire you actually have.

### Fail state
The window closed and nothing permanent was gained. Cyrus is now an ordinary civ with an army it cannot afford and grievances it cannot repay.

### Caveats
- Grievances accumulate permanently and invite coalitions. The cost is paid long after the war.
- The ten turns start at declaration, so any staging done after the DoW is time spent from the budget.
- Repeated surprise wars foreclose every diplomatic and city-state avenue for the rest of the game.

### Guide must account for
- A staging checklist that must fully complete before the declaration turn.
- A grievance budget tracked as a resource with a ceiling.
- A turn-10 checkpoint with an explicit abort-and-peace branch if the objective is not taken.
