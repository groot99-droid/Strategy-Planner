# Kupe — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Māori  |  **Leader Title:** Voyager of the Māori
**Source:** Gathering Storm

**Leader Ability — Kupe's Voyage**
Kupe begins the game embarked on the Ocean rather than on land, earning +2 Science and +2 Culture per turn before he founds his first city. Founding that first city grants a free Builder and +1 Population, and his Palace provides extra Housing and an Amenity, smoothing the unusual late, ocean-based start.

**Agenda — Kaitiakitanga**
Kupe preserves natural features and builds National Parks; he likes civilizations that respect the environment (avoiding harvesting and terrain removal) and dislikes those who despoil it.

**Civilization Ability — Mana**
The Māori start with Sailing and Shipbuilding and can move directly onto Ocean tiles from the outset. Their embarked units gain bonus Combat Strength and Movement, unimproved Woods and Rainforest tiles yield bonus Production, and Fishing Boats grant extra Food and trigger a Culture Bomb, making the Māori strong at settling and expanding across open ocean.

**Unique Units**
- Toa (replaces Swordsman) — A Classical era melee unit that can build the defensive Pā improvement on hills and weakens adjacent enemy units with a Haka war dance, giving the Māori solid mid-game defense despite their peaceful, exploration-focused kit.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Marae (District) — Replaces the Amphitheater in the Theater Square; it grants Culture and Faith from every passable feature (e.g., Woods, Floodplains) in the city and Tourism after Flight, rewarding a Māori empire that keeps its natural terrain intact.

### Historical Background
Kupe is a legendary Polynesian navigator credited in Māori oral tradition with being one of the first explorers to discover and name Aotearoa (New Zealand), guiding a great ocean-going waka (canoe) across the Pacific using wayfinding by stars, currents, and birds. As a figure of oral history rather than documented record, his exact dates are unknown, but he is central to Māori foundational narratives of migration and settlement. His in-game role as an ocean-starting leader who arrives to found cities directly mirrors this voyaging legend, and the Māori civilization's environmental, ocean-adjacency-focused kit reflects traditional Māori concepts of kaitiakitanga (guardianship of nature).

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Linear
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** No chopping, plus an ocean start with no immediate settle

> Standard era-block guides work. The ability amplifies a plan rather than supplying one, so the guide's real job is making the player choose a direction the amplifier can act on.

### Ancient / Classical
*Turns: 1 to 80*
- Ocean opening, then coastal settling on high-appeal land.
- Choose the victory path now. A permanent, unconditional bonus applied to no particular plan produces a comfortable game and no win.
- Respect the one constraint: No chopping, plus an ocean start with no immediate settle

### Medieval / Renaissance
*Turns: 80 to 160*
- Pristine terrain yields compounding.
- Era checkpoint: confirm the bonus is being spent, not banked. This is the characteristic failure of the shape.

### Industrial / Modern
*Turns: 160 to 240*
- National Parks and tourism.
- Era checkpoint: is the chosen victory path still reachable? Linear leaders can pivot; the flat curve makes it affordable.

### Atomic / Information
*Turns: 240 onward*
- Culture victory position.
- Close out the chosen condition.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Turn 150 with no victory path committed.
- Pick the path the accumulated empire is already closest to and commit fully. Linear leaders lose by drifting, not by collapsing.

### Fail state
A comfortable, undirected empire that never converts its advantage into a win condition.

### Caveats
- Starting at sea with no turn-one settle is a genuine tempo loss that must be planned around.
- Coastal cities are production-poor, which compounds with the no-chopping restriction.
- The appeal payoff arrives late while the costs are all paid early.

### Guide must account for
- An opening ocean route decision with a settle deadline attached.
- Explicit acknowledgment of the slow opening so players do not read it as failure.
- A production plan that assumes neither chopping nor strong land tiles.
