# Tamar — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Georgia  |  **Leader Title:** none
**Source:** Rise and Fall

**Leader Ability — Glory of the World, Kingdom and Faith**
Tamar gains Faith from combat victories based on the strength of the defeated unit, and after adopting Theology she can declare a special Protectorate War that doubles Faith output for a period and makes envoys sent to city-states that share Georgia's majority religion count double, tying military success to religious and diplomatic gains.

**Agenda — Narikala Fortress**
Tamar fortifies her cities with the strongest available Walls and dislikes civilizations that leave their cities poorly defended.

**Civilization Ability — Strength in Unity**
Georgia earns bonus Era Score for Dedications made at the start of Golden and Heroic Ages and gains extra Production toward building Walls, helping it weather the Loyalty and warfare pressures of the Rise and Fall systems.

**Unique Units**
- Khevsur (replaces Man-at-Arms) — A Medieval-era unit with a large Combat Strength bonus on Hills and no hill movement penalty, giving Georgia a strong defensive infantry option in its mountainous heartland.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Tsikhe (Building) — Replaces the Renaissance-era City Center walls upgrade with substantially higher Outer Defense Strength plus bonus Faith and Tourism, reinforcing Georgia's fortress-focused playstyle.

### Historical Background
Tamar the Great (reigned 1184-1213) presided over the political, military, and cultural high point of the medieval Kingdom of Georgia, expanding its borders through successful campaigns while overseeing a flourishing of Georgian art, architecture, and literature, including the national epic 'The Knight in the Panther's Skin.' She ruled from heavily fortified strongholds carved into the Caucasus mountains, such as the Narikala fortress overlooking Tbilisi, defending her kingdom against Seljuk and other regional powers. Her reign's reputation for both formidable defenses and religious devotion (Georgia being one of the world's oldest Christian states) is reflected in her fortress-building agenda and Faith-generating combat ability.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Monitor
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Conditional | **Condition cost:** Envoy and wall investment; Golden Age chain breaks if an era is missed

> The core loop is a stat check, not a build order. These guides fail hardest when written as sequences, because the player's real job is watching one number and responding to it.

### Setup
*Turns: 1 to roughly 25*

Establish the thing being monitored and the infrastructure that lets you respond to it.

- Name the watched stat on turn 1: Own era score approaching Golden Age threshold, AND ally-under-attack status
- Establish its bands before it matters, so the player recognizes trouble the first time rather than the third.
- Build the response capacity. A monitor with no available response is just anxiety.

### Standing Loop
*Turns: 25 to end of game*

This replaces the turn list entirely. The guide is this loop plus the era checkpoints.

- Read Own era score approaching Golden Age threshold, AND ally-under-attack status.
- Green band: continue current build. No action.
- Amber band: begin the correction now. Amber is the acting band; red is the failing band.
- Red band: the ability is off or about to be. Everything else is deprioritized until it clears.

### Era Checkpoints
*Turns: each era boundary*

Re-read the role. Monitor leaders change what the stat means across eras.

- Ancient/Classical: Walls and envoy investment. Slow start.
- Medieval/Renaissance: Golden Age chaining at full effect.
- Industrial/Modern: Strong while the chain holds, fragile if broken.
- Atomic/Information: Dependent on an unbroken sequence.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Watched stat sits in the red band for more than roughly 15 turns with no recovery path.
- Stop paying the Envoy and wall investment; Golden Age chain breaks if an era is missed. If the bonus is unreachable, its price is pure loss.
- Rebuild as a generic civ around whatever the monitored strategy already produced.

### Fail state
The stat crossed into red and stayed. For Tamar, Missing a single Golden Age breaks the compounding sequence and it does not restart at the same value.

### Caveats
- Missing a single Golden Age breaks the compounding sequence and it does not restart at the same value.
- Protectorate Wars require an ally to be attacked, which you do not control.
- Wall production is defensive spending that competes with everything else early.

### Guide must account for
- Era score as a monitored per-turn stat with a warning threshold before each era transition.
- Ally threat status watched continuously so Protectorate opportunities are not missed.
- A fallback plan for eras where no ally is attacked and no Golden Age is reachable.
