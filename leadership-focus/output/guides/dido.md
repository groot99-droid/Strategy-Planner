# Dido — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Phoenicia  |  **Leader Title:** none
**Source:** Gathering Storm

**Leader Ability — Founder of Carthage**
Cities with a Cothon can use a unique project to relocate Phoenicia's capital there, and Dido gains extra Trade Route capacity and a Production bonus toward Districts in the city containing her Government Plaza, supporting a highly mobile, expansion-driven playstyle.

**Agenda — Sicilian Wars**
Dido pursues coastal expansion for herself but dislikes rival civilizations that accumulate many coastal cities, echoing Carthage's historical rivalry with Greek colonies over Mediterranean coastlines.

**Civilization Ability — Mediterranean Colonies**
Coastal Phoenician cities founded on the same continent as the capital always have maximum Loyalty, embarked Settlers move faster and see farther with reduced embarkation costs, and Phoenicia starts with the Eureka for Writing already unlocked, making it exceptionally strong at rapid coastal settlement.

**Unique Units**
- Bireme (replaces Galley) — A faster, stronger naval melee unit that also shields nearby Traders from plundering, protecting Phoenicia's seaborne trade network.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Cothon (District) — Replaces the Harbor, boosts Production toward Settlers and naval units, heals naval units faster, and is required to relocate the Phoenician capital via Dido's leader ability.

### Historical Background
Dido is the legendary founder and first queen of Carthage, said in classical tradition (notably Virgil's Aeneid) to have fled Phoenician Tyre and founded her new city on the North African coast around the 9th century BC, growing it into one of the ancient Mediterranean's great maritime trading powers. Carthage's power rested on an extensive network of coastal colonies and trade routes stretching across the Mediterranean, a legacy that eventually put it in prolonged conflict with Greek city-states in Sicily and, later, with Rome in the Punic Wars. Her legendary founding of a new city by the sea and Phoenicia's historical mastery of coastal colonization directly shape both her capital-relocating leader ability and the civilization's emphasis on rapid, loyal coastal settlement.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Monitor
**Curve:** Flat | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Coastal settling; capital relocation has a real opportunity cost

> The core loop is a stat check, not a build order. These guides fail hardest when written as sequences, because the player's real job is watching one number and responding to it.

### Setup
*Turns: 1 to roughly 25*

Establish the thing being monitored and the infrastructure that lets you respond to it.

- Name the watched stat on turn 1: Loyalty pressure on newly settled/captured overseas cities pre-relocation
- Establish its bands before it matters, so the player recognizes trouble the first time rather than the third.
- Build the response capacity. A monitor with no available response is just anxiety.

### Standing Loop
*Turns: 25 to end of game*

This replaces the turn list entirely. The guide is this loop plus the era checkpoints.

- Read Loyalty pressure on newly settled/captured overseas cities pre-relocation.
- Green band: continue current build. No action.
- Amber band: begin the correction now. Amber is the acting band; red is the failing band.
- Red band: the ability is off or about to be. Everything else is deprioritized until it clears.

### Era Checkpoints
*Turns: each era boundary*

Re-read the role. Monitor leaders change what the stat means across eras.

- Ancient/Classical: Coastal settling and early forward positions.
- Medieval/Renaissance: Loyalty-free expansion onto new continents.
- Industrial/Modern: Wide multi-continental empire.
- Atomic/Information: Strong, broad position.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Watched stat sits in the red band for more than roughly 15 turns with no recovery path.
- Stop paying the Coastal settling; capital relocation has a real opportunity cost. If the bonus is unreachable, its price is pure loss.
- Rebuild as a generic civ around whatever the monitored strategy already produced.

### Fail state
The stat crossed into red and stayed. For Dido, Relocating the capital has real costs and cannot be undone casually.

### Caveats
- Relocating the capital has real costs and cannot be undone casually.
- Coastal cities are production-poor, which the loyalty immunity does not fix.
- Forward settling generates grievances from every civ you settle near.

### Guide must account for
- Capital relocation as a scheduled decision point with stated prerequisites, not an improvisation.
- A production supplement plan for coastal-heavy empires.
- A diplomatic cost budget for forward settling.
