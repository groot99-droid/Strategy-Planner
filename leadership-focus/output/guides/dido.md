# Dido — Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Monitor
**Curve:** Flat | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Coastal settling; capital relocation has a real opportunity cost

> The core loop is a stat check, not a build order. These guides fail hardest when written as sequences, because the player's real job is watching one number and responding to it.

## Setup
*Turns: 1 to roughly 25*

Establish the thing being monitored and the infrastructure that lets you respond to it.

- Name the watched stat on turn 1: Loyalty pressure on newly settled/captured overseas cities pre-relocation
- Establish its bands before it matters, so the player recognizes trouble the first time rather than the third.
- Build the response capacity. A monitor with no available response is just anxiety.

## Standing Loop
*Turns: 25 to end of game*

This replaces the turn list entirely. The guide is this loop plus the era checkpoints.

- Read Loyalty pressure on newly settled/captured overseas cities pre-relocation.
- Green band: continue current build. No action.
- Amber band: begin the correction now. Amber is the acting band; red is the failing band.
- Red band: the ability is off or about to be. Everything else is deprioritized until it clears.

## Era Checkpoints
*Turns: each era boundary*

Re-read the role. Monitor leaders change what the stat means across eras.

- Ancient/Classical: Coastal settling and early forward positions.
- Medieval/Renaissance: Loyalty-free expansion onto new continents.
- Industrial/Modern: Wide multi-continental empire.
- Atomic/Information: Strong, broad position.

## Abort branch
**Trigger:** Watched stat sits in the red band for more than roughly 15 turns with no recovery path.
- Stop paying the Coastal settling; capital relocation has a real opportunity cost. If the bonus is unreachable, its price is pure loss.
- Rebuild as a generic civ around whatever the monitored strategy already produced.

## Fail state
The stat crossed into red and stayed. For Dido, Relocating the capital has real costs and cannot be undone casually.

## Caveats
- Relocating the capital has real costs and cannot be undone casually.
- Coastal cities are production-poor, which the loyalty immunity does not fix.
- Forward settling generates grievances from every civ you settle near.

## Guide must account for
- Capital relocation as a scheduled decision point with stated prerequisites, not an improvisation.
- A production supplement plan for coastal-heavy empires.
- A diplomatic cost budget for forward settling.
