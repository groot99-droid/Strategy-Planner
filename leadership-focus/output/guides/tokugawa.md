# Tokugawa — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Japan  |  **Leader Title:** Tokugawa Ieyasu
**Source:** New Frontier Pass (Leader Pass persona)

**Leader Ability — Bakuhan**
Tokugawa's international Trade Routes suffer a penalty to all yields and Tourism, but his domestic Trade Routes grant bonus Culture, Science, and Gold for every specialty District at the destination, pushing Japan toward an inward-facing, isolationist economy built on strong domestic trade networks.

**Agenda — Sakoku**
Tokugawa wants to remain safe from conquest, favoring civilizations that likewise avoid war and expansion by conquest, and he dislikes leaders who have captured other civilizations' capitals.

**Civilization Ability — Meiji Restoration**
Japanese Districts receive standard rather than reduced adjacency bonuses even when adjacency conditions would normally give a lesser bonus (such as on Hills or next to other districts), letting Japan cluster districts tightly for strong yields; cities near the capital also gain Amenities, with Tourism from Districts unlocked later after researching Flight.

**Unique Units**
- Samurai (replaces Man-at-Arms) — A resilient melee unit that does not lose combat strength as it takes damage, letting it keep fighting effectively even when badly wounded.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Electronics Factory (Building) — Replaces the Factory and extends bonus Production and Culture to nearby cities rather than only its own city, strengthening Japan's tightly clustered industrial cities.

### Historical Background
Tokugawa Ieyasu (1543-1616) was the daimyo who emerged victorious from Japan's chaotic Sengoku ('Warring States') period after his decisive victory at the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600, founding the Tokugawa shogunate that would rule Japan for over 250 years. His government pursued a policy of strict social order and, under his successors, near-total national seclusion (sakoku), closing Japan off from most foreign trade and contact to preserve internal stability. Added to Civilization VI as an alternate persona for Japan alongside the base game's Hojo Tokimune, his in-game penalty to foreign trade paired with strong domestic trade bonuses, along with an agenda built around avoiding conquest, directly mirrors the historical isolationist Sakoku policy associated with his dynasty.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Linear
**Curve:** Flat | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** International trade heavily penalized; forfeits foreign trade income entirely

> Standard era-block guides work. The ability amplifies a plan rather than supplying one, so the guide's real job is making the player choose a direction the amplifier can act on.

### Ancient / Classical
*Turns: 1 to 80*
- Establishing the capital cluster and first internal routes.
- Choose the victory path now. A permanent, unconditional bonus applied to no particular plan produces a comfortable game and no win.
- Respect the one constraint: International trade heavily penalized; forfeits foreign trade income entirely

### Medieval / Renaissance
*Turns: 80 to 160*
- Domestic route yields stacking.
- Era checkpoint: confirm the bonus is being spent, not banked. This is the characteristic failure of the shape.

### Industrial / Modern
*Turns: 160 to 240*
- Strong self-contained economy.
- Era checkpoint: is the chosen victory path still reachable? Linear leaders can pivot; the flat curve makes it affordable.

### Atomic / Information
*Turns: 240 onward*
- Solid, insulated.
- Close out the chosen condition.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Turn 150 with no victory path committed.
- Pick the path the accumulated empire is already closest to and commit fully. Linear leaders lose by drifting, not by collapsing.

### Fail state
A comfortable, undirected empire that never converts its advantage into a win condition.

### Caveats
- International trade income is forfeited entirely, not reduced. That is a whole mechanic given away.
- Domestic routes need many cities to be worth much, so the kit rewards width you must build first.
- Isolation reduces diplomatic leverage and information about rival progress.

### Guide must account for
- City count treated as the multiplier on the entire trade strategy.
- An explicit prohibition on international routes, since they will look tempting.
- An information plan, because isolation means flying blind on rival progress.
