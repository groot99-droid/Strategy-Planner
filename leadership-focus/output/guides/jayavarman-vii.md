# Jayavarman VII — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Khmer  |  **Leader Title:** none
**Source:** New Frontier Pass

**Leader Ability — Monasteries of the King**
Jayavarman's Holy Sites gain a strong adjacency bonus from Rivers, provide Housing when built on a River, generate Food equal to their total adjacency bonus, and trigger a Culture Bomb on nearby unowned tiles when built. This lets Khmer cities grow huge around river-adjacent Holy Sites while also expanding borders for free.

**Agenda — An End to Suffering**
Jayavarman likes civilizations that build Holy Sites and maintain a high average city population, and dislikes those that neglect religion or keep small, undeveloped cities.

**Civilization Ability — Grand Barays**
Khmer Aqueducts grant bonus Faith and an Amenity in addition to their normal Housing bonus, and adjacent Farms receive extra Food, making Aqueduct placement a key part of Khmer city planning.

**Unique Units**
- Domrey (replaces Trebuchet) — An elephant-mounted ballista that can move and fire in the same turn and exerts a zone of control, making it far more mobile and threatening than the siege unit it replaces.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Prasat (Building) — Replaces the Temple, adds a Relic Great Work slot, and lets Missionaries killed in religious combat generate a Relic, tying nicely into a religious/culture hybrid strategy.

### Historical Background
Jayavarman VII (reigned c. 1181-1218) was the most powerful ruler of the Khmer Empire, expanding it to its greatest territorial extent from the capital of Angkor Thom in present-day Cambodia. A devout Mahayana Buddhist, he launched an unprecedented building program of temples, hospitals, roads, and reservoirs (barays), including the Bayon temple with its enormous carved stone faces. His combination of massive water-management infrastructure and prolific religious construction is directly reflected in the Khmer's in-game focus on Holy Sites, Aqueducts, and river-based city growth.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Linear
**Curve:** Flat | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** River dependency

> Standard era-block guides work. The ability amplifies a plan rather than supplying one, so the guide's real job is making the player choose a direction the amplifier can act on.

### Ancient / Classical
*Turns: 1 to 80*
- River Holy Sites providing Food, Housing, and Faith at once.
- Choose the victory path now. A permanent, unconditional bonus applied to no particular plan produces a comfortable game and no win.
- Respect the one constraint: River dependency

### Medieval / Renaissance
*Turns: 80 to 160*
- Population engine compounding.
- Era checkpoint: confirm the bonus is being spent, not banked. This is the characteristic failure of the shape.

### Industrial / Modern
*Turns: 160 to 240*
- Large high-population empire.
- Era checkpoint: is the chosen victory path still reachable? Linear leaders can pivot; the flat curve makes it affordable.

### Atomic / Information
*Turns: 240 onward*
- Flexible; population supports any path.
- Close out the chosen condition.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Turn 150 with no victory path committed.
- Pick the path the accumulated empire is already closest to and commit fully. Linear leaders lose by drifting, not by collapsing.

### Fail state
A comfortable, undirected empire that never converts its advantage into a win condition.

### Caveats
- River dependency constrains settling more than it appears, especially for later cities.
- Flooding threatens exactly the tiles the strategy is built on.
- Holy Site placement for the river bonus can conflict with optimal adjacency for other districts.

### Guide must account for
- River-first settle planning across the whole intended empire, not just the capital.
- Aqueduct and dam sequencing relative to Holy Site placement.
- A victory path chosen separately, since population is an input rather than a direction.
