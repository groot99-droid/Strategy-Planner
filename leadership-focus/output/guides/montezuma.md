# Montezuma — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Aztec  |  **Leader Title:** The Boiling Lake
**Source:** Base Game

**Leader Ability — Gifts for the Tlatoani**
Luxury resources improved in Aztec territory grant an Amenity to two extra cities, and Montezuma's military (and religious) units gain Combat Strength on attack for each different luxury resource he has improved, making luxury acquisition a direct combat multiplier.

**Agenda — Tlatoani**
Montezuma likes civilizations that possess the same luxury resources he does, and dislikes those who hold luxuries he lacks.

**Civilization Ability — Legend of the Five Suns**
Builders can be used to speed up (rush) the construction of Districts, letting the Aztecs convert worker labor directly into faster district growth.

**Unique Units**
- Eagle Warrior (replaces Warrior) — A stronger melee unit than the standard Warrior that can capture defeated enemy units and turn them into Builders, fueling the civ's district-rushing ability.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Tlachtli (Building) — Provides Faith, Culture, an Amenity from Entertainment, and Great General points, giving the Aztecs strong early culture and faith output from a military-adjacent building.

### Historical Background
Montezuma (Moctezuma II) was the ninth tlatoani (emperor) of the Aztec Empire, ruling from 1502 until 1520 at the height of its territorial power, commanding a tribute-based empire across central Mexico. He is best known historically for his fateful encounter with Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés in 1519, which set in motion the fall of Tenochtitlan and the Aztec Empire within two years. Aztec economic and religious life depended heavily on luxury goods and tribute, which is reflected in his in-game ability tying luxury resources directly to happiness and combat strength.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Countdown
**Curve:** Spike | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Combat strength scales with luxury variety, so needs wide territory
**Convert by:** turn 90

> The ability has an expiry. The guide is a timer wrapped around a conversion deadline. Its most important content is not the build order, it is the turn the plan stops and what replaces it.

### Phase 1 — Arm
*Turns: 1 to roughly 30*

Reach the window with everything already staged. Nothing in this phase is optional; staging done after the window opens is spent from the budget.

- Turn 1: identify the target. Montezuma's window is short enough that target selection cannot wait for scouting to finish.
- Turns 1-15: scout toward the target only. Exploration away from the target is a luxury this shape cannot afford.
- Turns 10-30: production goes to the units or charges the window will consume. Infrastructure that pays back after 90 is deferred.
- Pre-window checkpoint: confirm Combat strength scales with luxury variety, so needs wide territory is satisfied and units are within striking distance, not at home.

### Phase 2 — Window
*Turns: roughly 30 to 90*

Convert tempo into permanent assets. Cities, wonders, or improvements. Not kills, not gold, not map presence.


**Checkpoints:**
- *midpoint of window*: Is at least half the objective secured? → if yes: proceed; if no: Reduce scope now. Take one city instead of three. Partial conversion beats total expiry.
- *90*: Has the tempo become something permanent? → if yes: Proceed to Phase 3.; if no: Abort branch.

### Phase 3 — Digest
*Turns: 90 onward*

Stabilize what was taken. This phase is longer than both prior phases combined and most guides for this shape underwrite it badly.

- Amenity and loyalty audit on every acquired city before any new construction.
- Infrastructure catch-up: the arming phase deliberately skipped this, and the bill comes due now.
- Choose a victory path. Montezuma has no leader-specific direction from here; Luxury combat bonus diluted by inflated base strength values.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Window closed with no permanent gain, or three stalled turns before the midpoint.
- Make peace immediately. Prolonging a failed window converts a bad position into a lost game.
- Disband surplus units. The maintenance is now pure loss.
- Switch to the generic development plan for the empire you actually have.

### Fail state
The window closed and nothing permanent was gained. Montezuma is now an ordinary civ with an army it cannot afford and grievances it cannot repay.

### Caveats
- Captured Builders are worthless without a queued improvement plan to spend charges on.
- The combat bonus counts distinct luxuries, not copies. Ten of one resource is one bonus.
- Warring everyone removes the trade partners you need to acquire luxuries you lack.

### Guide must account for
- A Builder-charge sink list maintained in parallel with the war plan.
- Luxury acquisition tracked as its own objective line, separate from conquest.
- A diplomacy floor: which civs must stay peaceful to keep the luxury count climbing.
