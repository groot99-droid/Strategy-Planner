# Peter — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Russia  |  **Leader Title:** Tsar of Russia
**Source:** Base Game

**Leader Ability — The Grand Embassy**
When Russia has an active Trade Route to a civilization that is significantly more advanced in technology or civics, Peter gains bonus Science and Culture from that route (scaling with how far behind Russia is), incentivizing Russia to trade with — and catch up to — more advanced neighbors early on.

**Agenda — Westernizer**
Peter is friendly toward civilizations that are ahead of him in technology and civics, and dislikes civilizations that are behind, reflecting his historical drive to modernize Russia along Western European lines.

**Civilization Ability — Mother Russia**
Russian cities claim extra surrounding territory the moment they are founded and gain bonus Faith and Production from Tundra tiles; in Gathering Storm, Russian units are also immune to Blizzard damage while enemies in Russian territory take extra Blizzard damage, letting Russia thrive in its characteristic cold, tundra-heavy homeland.

**Unique Units**
- Cossack (replaces Cavalry) — A strong Industrial era cavalry unit with bonus Combat Strength against wounded units, well-suited to finishing off weakened enemies.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Lavra (District) — Replaces the Holy Site at lower production cost; generates strong Great Prophet points (plus Great Writer/Artist/Musician points from its worship buildings) and expands the city's borders whenever a Great Person is activated there, making Russia excellent for religious and cultural strategies.

### Historical Background
Peter I ('Peter the Great', 1672-1725) ruled Russia (first as Tsar, later as Emperor) and pursued an aggressive campaign to modernize and Westernize the country, including a famous 1697-98 'Grand Embassy' tour of Western Europe — traveling incognito to study shipbuilding, military technology, and administration — that directly inspires his in-game leader ability and 'Westernizer' agenda. He founded St. Petersburg as a new, Western-facing capital, built Russia's first modern navy, and reformed the army, bureaucracy, and calendar. His reign's expansionist, cold-climate character (fighting the Great Northern War partly in harsh winter conditions) is echoed by Russia's tundra-friendly, territory-grabbing civilization ability.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Gate
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Tundra dependency

> The strategy does not begin on a turn. It begins on an unlock. This needs two complete guides with a trigger between them, not one guide with a note in the middle.

### Pre-Gate
*Turns: 1 until the gate opens*

Survive, develop, and stage. This is a real phase with its own complete build order, not a waiting room.

- State the gate explicitly on turn 1 so the player knows what they are working toward: A tundra-compatible pantheon secured (contested — race it)
- Give an estimated arrival turn AND the research or civic path that gets there fastest.
- Build the prerequisites the gate assumes but does not provide. Tundra dependency
- Maintain a defensive floor. The gate does not protect you while you wait for it.

### Gate Check
*Turns: variable*

A single decision point, not a phase.


### Post-Gate
*Turns: gate onward*

The actual leader strategy, now live.

- Great Writers, Artists, and Musicians accumulating rapidly.
- Run the closing-window check: most Gate abilities are also time-limited on the far end. Identify when parity arrives.
- Convert before parity. Permanent

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Gate opens with staging incomplete and a rival is already at parity.
- Do not open the strategy. Playing an unstaged Gate leader into a prepared opponent is the worst outcome available.
- Fold into a generic plan built on whatever the pre-gate phase produced.

### Fail state
The gate opened, the window closed, and the strategy never ran. The pre-gate phase was treated as filler and produced nothing usable.

### Caveats
- Tundra food scarcity caps city growth, which caps everything downstream.
- The pantheon that makes tundra Faith work is contested and can be taken.
- Lavra Great Person points still need Faith to convert into actual Great People.

### Guide must account for
- A pantheon race deadline with a fallback pantheon if the preferred one is taken.
- A food strategy for tundra cities, since the terrain does not provide one.
- Faith allocation split between Great People purchase and religious spread.
