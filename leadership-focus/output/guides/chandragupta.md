# Chandragupta — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** India  |  **Leader Title:** Founder of the Maurya Empire
**Source:** Rise and Fall

**Leader Ability — Arthashastra**
After researching Military Training, Chandragupta can declare a War of Territorial Expansion, granting his units extra Movement and Combat Strength for the following turns — a controlled way to seize nearby territory quickly.

**Agenda — Maurya Empire**
Chandragupta seeks to expand his own empire and dislikes other civilizations that settle cities too close to his borders.

**Civilization Ability — Dharma**
Indian cities gain the Follower Beliefs of every religion present among their population, not just the majority religion. In Gathering Storm this also grants an Amenity for each religion with at least one follower and boosts Missionary spreads and Trade Route religious pressure, making India uniquely effective at multi-religion cities.

**Unique Units**
- Varu (replaces Knight) — An elephant-mounted heavy cavalry unit that causes nearby enemy units to become fearful (reducing their effectiveness) and resists the usual counters to cavalry, well-suited to India's defensive-leaning playstyle.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Stepwell (Improvement) — A unique tile improvement that boosts Faith and Housing/growth-related yields, particularly valuable on dry terrain, supporting India's strong religious and population growth focus.

### Historical Background
Chandragupta Maurya founded the Maurya Empire around 321 BCE, unifying most of the Indian subcontinent for the first time in history with guidance from his strategist advisor Chanakya, traditionally credited as author of the Arthashastra — the classical treatise on statecraft and military strategy directly named in Chandragupta's leader ability. His empire laid the foundation later expanded and famously ruled with a turn toward Buddhist pacifism by his grandson, Ashoka. India's long history as home to numerous major religions living alongside one another is reflected in the civ ability Dharma, which rewards accommodating multiple faiths within the same cities rather than enforcing religious uniformity.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Gate
**Curve:** Spike | **Conversion:** Expiring | **Condition cost:** Only fires on Wars of Territorial Expansion, so needs an adjacent target
**Convert by:** turn 100

> The strategy does not begin on a turn. It begins on an unlock. This needs two complete guides with a trigger between them, not one guide with a note in the middle.

### Pre-Gate
*Turns: 1 until the gate opens*

Survive, develop, and stage. This is a real phase with its own complete build order, not a waiting room.

- State the gate explicitly on turn 1 so the player knows what they are working toward: A valid casus belli for a War of Territorial Expansion against an adjacent civ
- Give an estimated arrival turn AND the research or civic path that gets there fastest.
- Build the prerequisites the gate assumes but does not provide. Only fires on Wars of Territorial Expansion, so needs an adjacent target
- Maintain a defensive floor. The gate does not protect you while you wait for it.

### Gate Check
*Turns: variable*

A single decision point, not a phase.


### Post-Gate
*Turns: gate onward*

The actual leader strategy, now live.

- Peak, in narrow bursts against adjacent targets.
- Run the closing-window check: most Gate abilities are also time-limited on the far end. Identify when parity arrives.
- Convert before parity. Expiring

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Gate opens with staging incomplete and a rival is already at parity.
- Do not open the strategy. Playing an unstaged Gate leader into a prepared opponent is the worst outcome available.
- Fold into a generic plan built on whatever the pre-gate phase produced.

### Fail state
The gate opened, the window closed, and the strategy never ran. The pre-gate phase was treated as filler and produced nothing usable.

### Caveats
- The bonus requires a specific casus belli, so the real start turn is a civic unlock, not turn one.
- The target must be adjacent. A distant neighbor makes the whole kit inapplicable.
- Ten turns is the entire window, and it is spent moving as much as fighting.

### Guide must account for
- The civic unlock as the actual opening of the plan, with a defined pre-gate build order.
- Pre-positioning completed before the war declaration.
- A target viability check based on distance, run before committing to the strategy.
