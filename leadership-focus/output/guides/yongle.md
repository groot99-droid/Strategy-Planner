# Yongle — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** China  |  **Leader Title:** The Yongle Emperor
**Source:** New Frontier Pass (Leader Pass persona)

**Leader Ability — Lijia**
Yongle's cities gain access to unique Lijia projects that convert a share of Production into Food, Faith, or Gold, and cities with high Population generate extra Gold, Science, and Culture per Citizen, rewarding large, well-developed Chinese cities.

**Agenda — Yinding**
Yongle favors civilizations running a positive Gold income and dislikes those running a Gold deficit.

**Civilization Ability — Dynastic Cycle**
China's Eurekas and Inspirations provide a larger boost toward the cost of the technology or civic they unlock, and completing a Wonder grants a free Eureka and Inspiration, accelerating China's overall tech and civic progress.

**Unique Units**
- Crouching Tiger Cannon (replaces Bombard) — An early gunpowder siege unit that does not require the Niter resource normally needed by the Bombard, letting China field powerful siege weapons without a strategic resource dependency.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Great Wall (Improvement) — A unique tile improvement built on Hills that provides early defense and Gold, later converting to Culture and Tourism, and gains bonuses for connecting to adjacent Great Wall segments.

### Historical Background
The Yongle Emperor (reigned 1402-1424) was the third emperor of China's Ming dynasty, who seized the throne from his nephew and went on to relocate the capital to Beijing, where he commissioned the Forbidden City. He commissioned the enormous 'Yongle Encyclopedia' compiling China's accumulated knowledge, expanded and fortified sections of the Great Wall, and sponsored Admiral Zheng He's famous treasure-fleet voyages that projected Chinese naval power and trade across the Indian Ocean. Yongle is presented in Civilization VI as an alternate persona for China alongside the base game's Qin Shi Huang, added later via the Leader Pass DLC program, with his in-game focus on Production-conversion projects and large, wealthy cities reflecting his ambitious capital-building and encyclopedic era.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Gate
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Project turns spent reaching pop thresholds are turns not spent building

> The strategy does not begin on a turn. It begins on an unlock. This needs two complete guides with a trigger between them, not one guide with a note in the middle.

### Pre-Gate
*Turns: 1 until the gate opens*

Survive, develop, and stage. This is a real phase with its own complete build order, not a waiting room.

- State the gate explicitly on turn 1 so the player knows what they are working toward: A city crosses population 10 via the Lijia project
- Give an estimated arrival turn AND the research or civic path that gets there fastest.
- Build the prerequisites the gate assumes but does not provide. Project turns spent reaching pop thresholds are turns not spent building
- Maintain a defensive floor. The gate does not protect you while you wait for it.

### Gate Check
*Turns: variable*

A single decision point, not a phase.


### Post-Gate
*Turns: gate onward*

The actual leader strategy, now live.

- Cities crossing the threshold; yields step up sharply.
- Run the closing-window check: most Gate abilities are also time-limited on the far end. Identify when parity arrives.
- Convert before parity. Permanent

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Gate opens with staging incomplete and a rival is already at parity.
- Do not open the strategy. Playing an unstaged Gate leader into a prepared opponent is the worst outcome available.
- Fold into a generic plan built on whatever the pre-gate phase produced.

### Fail state
The gate opened, the window closed, and the strategy never ran. The pre-gate phase was treated as filler and produced nothing usable.

### Caveats
- The payoff is threshold-gated, so progress genuinely looks like nothing until it looks like everything.
- Projects consume production that could otherwise be building the housing needed to reach the threshold.
- Housing and amenities must be solved before the projects are worth running.

### Guide must account for
- Housing and food infrastructure sequenced before project spam, not alongside it.
- Per-city threshold tracking, since cities cross at different times.
- An explicit note that the pre-threshold flatline is expected.
