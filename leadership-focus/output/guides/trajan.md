# Trajan — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Roman  |  **Leader Title:** Optimus Princeps ("Best Ruler")
**Source:** Base Game

**Leader Ability — Trajan's Column**
Every city Trajan founds or acquires gets an extra free building in its City Center slot beyond the normal one (a Monument if still in the Ancient Era, or the era-appropriate equivalent later), giving Rome a permanent head start on infrastructure in every city, new or old.

**Agenda — Optimus Princeps**
Trajan tries to bring as much territory as possible under his empire's control and looks down on civilizations that hold only a small amount of territory.

**Civilization Ability — All Roads Lead to Rome**
All Roman cities start with a free Trading Post, roads are automatically built to new cities within trade range of the Capital, and Gold income from Trade Routes passing through Roman cities is increased, giving Rome an economic and connectivity edge from the earliest turns.

**Unique Units**
- Legion (replaces Swordsman) — A stronger, more expensive Classical-era melee unit that doesn't require Iron and carries a builder charge it can spend to construct a Roman Fort, build a road, or harvest a resource in the field.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Bath (District) — Replaces the Aqueduct (must be built adjacent to the City Center and a water/mountain feature) and provides Amenities alongside its Housing bonus, making Rome's early cities more comfortable to grow.

### Historical Background
Trajan ruled the Roman Empire from 98 to 117 AD and pushed it to its greatest territorial extent, conquering Dacia (for its gold) and campaigning into Mesopotamia and Armenia against Parthia. He is remembered as one of the 'Five Good Emperors' for combining military conquest with an ambitious building program: Trajan's Forum, Trajan's Market, and Trajan's Column (commemorating the Dacian Wars) all still stand in Rome today. His reputation for expanding territory while simultaneously building up infrastructure at home directly informs his in-game kit, which rewards both extra city buildings and holding maximal territory.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Linear
**Curve:** Flat | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** None

> Standard era-block guides work. The ability amplifies a plan rather than supplying one, so the guide's real job is making the player choose a direction the amplifier can act on.

### Ancient / Classical
*Turns: 1 to 80*
- Aggressive settling with free culture in every city.
- Choose the victory path now. A permanent, unconditional bonus applied to no particular plan produces a comfortable game and no win.
- Respect the one constraint: None

### Medieval / Renaissance
*Turns: 80 to 160*
- Compounding civic lead from accumulated Monuments.
- Era checkpoint: confirm the bonus is being spent, not banked. This is the characteristic failure of the shape.

### Industrial / Modern
*Turns: 160 to 240*
- Ordinary large empire; ability contribution is baked in already.
- Era checkpoint: is the chosen victory path still reachable? Linear leaders can pivot; the flat curve makes it affordable.

### Atomic / Information
*Turns: 240 onward*
- No ongoing effect, but the head start persists.
- Close out the chosen condition.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Turn 150 with no victory path committed.
- Pick the path the accumulated empire is already closest to and commit fully. Linear leaders lose by drifting, not by collapsing.

### Fail state
A comfortable, undirected empire that never converts its advantage into a win condition.

### Caveats
- Free Monuments make expansion feel free. Amenities, gold, and loyalty are still real limits.
- Automatic roads move invaders through your territory as efficiently as they move Legions.
- The bonus is front-loaded per city, so late-founded cities contribute much less.

### Guide must account for
- A settle-rate cap tied to amenity and gold capacity rather than available land.
- A culture spend plan, since the advantage is worthless unbanked.
- Diminishing returns on cities founded after the culture lead is already established.
