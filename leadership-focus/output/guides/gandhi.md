# Gandhi — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** India  |  **Leader Title:** none
**Source:** Base Game

**Leader Ability — Satyagraha**
For every civilization India has met that has founded a religion and is not at war with India, India gains bonus Faith; civilizations that go to war with India suffer an Amenities penalty, rewarding India for staying peaceful while religions spread around the world.

**Agenda — Peacekeeper**
Gandhi avoids starting wars or actions that would brand him a warmonger, likes other leaders who keep the peace, and dislikes aggressive, war-prone civilizations.

**Civilization Ability — Dharma**
Indian cities gain the Follower Beliefs of every religion present within them rather than just the majority religion; from Gathering Storm onward India also gains an Amenity per religion present, extra Missionary spreads, and stronger religious pressure from Trade Routes, making India a religious-diversity powerhouse.

**Unique Units**
- Varu (replaces Horseman) — A war-elephant unit that ignores enemy Zones of Control and reduces the combat strength of adjacent enemy units, making it a strong early anti-cavalry and area-denial unit.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Stepwell (Improvement) — A unique tile improvement that provides Housing and Food, with bonus Food next to Farms and bonus Faith next to Holy Sites, supporting India's tall, high-population playstyle.

### Historical Background
Mohandas 'Mahatma' Gandhi (1869-1948) led India's independence movement from British colonial rule through a philosophy of nonviolent civil disobedience, or satyagraha ('truth-force'), that inspired later civil rights movements worldwide. A devout advocate of religious pluralism and tolerance between India's Hindu and Muslim communities, he was assassinated in 1948 shortly after independence was achieved. His commitment to nonviolence and interfaith coexistence is directly named in his leader ability (Satyagraha) and echoed in India's civilization ability, which rewards embracing many religions rather than enforcing one.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Monitor
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Conditional | **Condition cost:** Must stay at peace; scales with other civs founding religions

> The core loop is a stat check, not a build order. These guides fail hardest when written as sequences, because the player's real job is watching one number and responding to it.

### Setup
*Turns: 1 to roughly 25*

Establish the thing being monitored and the infrastructure that lets you respond to it.

- Name the watched stat on turn 1: Number of met civs with an active religion, and current war/peace status with each
- Establish its bands before it matters, so the player recognizes trouble the first time rather than the third.
- Build the response capacity. A monitor with no available response is just anxiety.

### Standing Loop
*Turns: 25 to end of game*

This replaces the turn list entirely. The guide is this loop plus the era checkpoints.

- Read Number of met civs with an active religion, and current war/peace status with each.
- Green band: continue current build. No action.
- Amber band: begin the correction now. Amber is the acting band; red is the failing band.
- Red band: the ability is off or about to be. Everything else is deprioritized until it clears.

### Era Checkpoints
*Turns: each era boundary*

Re-read the role. Monitor leaders change what the stat means across eras.

- Ancient/Classical: Exploration and first contact. Faith scales with civs met.
- Medieval/Renaissance: Passive Faith accumulating from peaceful relations.
- Industrial/Modern: Strong Faith economy contingent on continued peace.
- Atomic/Information: Same, increasingly fragile as wars become likelier.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Watched stat sits in the red band for more than roughly 15 turns with no recovery path.
- Stop paying the Must stay at peace; scales with other civs founding religions. If the bonus is unreachable, its price is pure loss.
- Rebuild as a generic civ around whatever the monitored strategy already produced.

### Fail state
The stat crossed into red and stayed. For Gandhi, Output depends on how many rivals founded religions, which you do not control.

### Caveats
- Output depends on how many rivals founded religions, which you do not control.
- Each war zeroes that civ's contribution, so a single conflict has an ongoing economic cost.
- Civs you have not met contribute nothing, making early exploration an economic activity.

### Guide must account for
- Exploration prioritized early and treated as income generation, not scouting.
- A strictly defensive military doctrine with rules for what counts as provocation.
- A contingency for games where few rivals found religions.
