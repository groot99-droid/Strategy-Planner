# Cleopatra (Ptolemaic) — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Egypt  |  **Leader Title:** Pharaoh of the Ptolemaic Kingdom
**Source:** New Frontier Pass (Persona Packs) for the base 'Cleopatra' persona; Ptolemaic Cleopatra specifically is a Leader Pass persona (Rulers of the Sahara Pack)

**Leader Ability — Arrival of Hapi**
As the Ptolemaic persona (added later via the Leader Pass' Rulers of the Sahara Pack), Cleopatra's ability grants resources on Floodplains +1 Food and +1 Culture, and Floodplains in her territory grant adjacent tiles +1 Appeal instead of the usual -1. (Her original, base-game persona instead uses the ability Mediterranean's Bride, which gives bonus Gold on Egypt's outgoing Trade Routes and bonus Food/Gold on incoming ones.)

**Agenda — Basilike Ge**
As Ptolemaic Cleopatra, she favors civilizations with high Food output and dislikes those with low Food output. (Her original persona's agenda, Queen of the Nile, instead favors leaders with powerful militaries and dislikes weak ones.)

**Civilization Ability — Iteru**
Egypt builds Wonders and districts faster when adjacent to a river, and uniquely can construct Wonders and districts directly on Floodplains, letting Egypt turn its floodplain-heavy river valleys into dense wonder- and district-packed cities.

**Unique Units**
- Maryannu Chariot Archer (replaces Heavy Chariot (Horseman-tier ranged cavalry)) — A fast Ancient era ranged cavalry unit with strong Ranged Strength and extra Movement on open terrain, giving Egypt an unusually powerful early rush/exploration unit.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Sphinx (Improvement) — A unique tile improvement giving Faith and Culture (with a bonus when adjacent to a Wonder), and Tourism after Flight, reinforcing Egypt's wonder- and culture-focused playstyle.

### Historical Background
Cleopatra VII Thea Philopator (69-30 BCE) was the last active ruler of Egypt's Ptolemaic dynasty, a Greek Macedonian line established after Alexander the Great's conquests, ruling Egypt for nearly three centuries before Roman annexation. Fluent, politically astute, and famed for her alliances with Julius Caesar and Mark Antony, she worked to preserve Egyptian independence against expanding Roman power, ultimately losing at the Battle of Actium. Civ VI splits her into two personas reflecting this hybrid identity: a base-game 'Egyptian' Cleopatra evoking the older pharaonic tradition and river/floodplain economy of the Nile, and a Leader Pass 'Ptolemaic' persona (Arrival of Hapi, named for the Egyptian god of the Nile flood) emphasizing her Hellenistic-Egyptian royal household and its farmland (basilike ge, 'royal land').

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Linear
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** River and floodplain dependency

> Standard era-block guides work. The ability amplifies a plan rather than supplying one, so the guide's real job is making the player choose a direction the amplifier can act on.

### Ancient / Classical
*Turns: 1 to 80*
- River and floodplain settling with early Food and Culture.
- Choose the victory path now. A permanent, unconditional bonus applied to no particular plan produces a comfortable game and no win.
- Respect the one constraint: River and floodplain dependency

### Medieval / Renaissance
*Turns: 80 to 160*
- Appeal advantage compounding into culture.
- Era checkpoint: confirm the bonus is being spent, not banked. This is the characteristic failure of the shape.

### Industrial / Modern
*Turns: 160 to 240*
- National Parks and Seaside Resorts on inverted-appeal terrain.
- Era checkpoint: is the chosen victory path still reachable? Linear leaders can pivot; the flat curve makes it affordable.

### Atomic / Information
*Turns: 240 onward*
- Strong tourism position.
- Close out the chosen condition.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Turn 150 with no victory path committed.
- Pick the path the accumulated empire is already closest to and commit fully. Linear leaders lose by drifting, not by collapsing.

### Fail state
A comfortable, undirected empire that never converts its advantage into a win condition.

### Caveats
- Floodplains still flood. The appeal inversion does not prevent the damage.
- Dam construction competes for production exactly when growth is most valuable.
- River dependency constrains settling more tightly than it first appears.

### Guide must account for
- A flood risk versus appeal tradeoff decision per city, with dam timing attached.
- The two-phase nature of the payoff, since early Food and late Parks need different plans.
- River availability assessed at settle time across the whole intended empire.
