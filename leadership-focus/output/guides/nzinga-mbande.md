# Nzinga Mbande — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Kongolese  |  **Leader Title:** Queen of Ndongo and Matamba
**Source:** New Frontier Pass (Leader Pass persona)

**Leader Ability — Queen of Ndongo and Matamba**
All Kongolese cities on the same continent as the Capital (including the Capital itself) get a bonus to all yields, while any cities founded on other continents instead suffer a yield penalty, strongly pushing Nzinga toward staying concentrated on her home continent rather than overseas colonization.

**Agenda — Decolonization**
Nzinga favors civilizations that have not settled any cities on her home continent, and dislikes those that have established a foothold there, reflecting her historical resistance to foreign colonization.

**Civilization Ability — Nkisi**
Kongo gets bonus Food, Production, Faith, and Gold from Relics, Artifacts, and Sculptures (including Heroic Relics), earns Great Artist, Great Musician, and Great Merchant points faster, and its Palace can hold extra Great Works, making Kongo a strong Culture/Great Works-focused civilization.

**Unique Units**
- Ngao Mbeba (replaces Swordsman) — A stronger melee unit that avoids movement/sight penalties in Woods and Rainforest and gets a defensive bonus against ranged attacks, well suited to fighting through and defending Kongo's forested homeland.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Mbanza (District) — Replaces the Neighborhood; unlocked earlier (with Guilds instead of Urbanization) and grants Housing, Food, and Gold, but can only be built on Woods or Rainforest tiles, matching Kongo's historical jungle-based settlements.

### Historical Background
Nzinga Mbande ruled the Central African kingdoms of Ndongo and later Matamba (in modern Angola) for roughly four decades in the 17th century, becoming one of the most formidable diplomats and military strategists to resist European colonization in her era. She skillfully played the Portuguese against rival African and Dutch powers, converted to Christianity as a political tool when useful, built alliances with escaped slaves and runaway soldiers to bolster her forces, and successfully kept her kingdoms independent of Portuguese control for most of her reign. Her lifelong struggle to keep foreign powers out of her home territory while she herself avoided overseas entanglement is the direct historical basis for her in-game ability and 'Decolonization' agenda, both of which reward staying concentrated on one's home continent and punish rivals who colonize it.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Front-Load
**Curve:** Flat | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Continent-locked; overseas cities are actively bad

> Nearly every decision that matters is made in the first twenty turns and cannot be reversed. Guide density should be inverted: dense at the start, sparse after.

### Turn 0 — Assessment
*Turns: before the first Settler moves*

A genuine go or no-go on the start position. This phase exists only in this shape and it is the most valuable thing in the template.

- Check the hard requirement: Continent-locked; overseas cities are actively bad
- If the requirement is absent within a reasonable walk, decide now whether to move the Settler or play the leader as a generic civ. Deciding this at turn 40 is deciding it too late.
- Estimate the ceiling. For radius- and continent-capped leaders, the ceiling is knowable early and determines the victory path.

### Turns 1-20 — Commit
*Turns: 1 to 20*

Make every irreversible decision deliberately rather than incidentally.

- Draw the full settle map before the second Settler is produced, including sites you will not reach for 40 turns.
- Assign district roles to each city site at settle time, not at build time. For placement-restricted leaders this is the whole game.
- Mark each decision with its reversibility. The guide should say plainly which choices are permanent.
- A continent survey prioritized early, since it determines the size of the whole game.

### Turns 20-80 — Execute
*Turns: 20 to 80*

Build out the plan drawn in the commit phase. Low decision density by design.

- Follow the settle map. Deviation is the common failure, and it usually feels reasonable at the time.
- Consolidated continental empire with stacked yields.

### Turn 80+ — Live With It
*Turns: 80 onward*

Operate inside the ceiling set at turn 20.

- Choose a victory path that fits the ceiling rather than fighting it.
- Ceiling determined by continent size, which was fixed at map generation.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Turn 0 assessment fails and no acceptable alternative start is within reach.
- Play the leader as a generic civ and say so explicitly. A named fallback is better than a strategy that quietly cannot work.
- Target a faster victory, since the capped ceiling will not win a long game.

### Fail state
A placement decision made before turn 20 has capped the game, and the cap will not be visible until roughly turn 100. A small home continent is a permanent low ceiling with no workaround.

### Caveats
- A small home continent is a permanent low ceiling with no workaround.
- Overseas expansion is actively bad, which removes an entire mid-game option.
- You cannot know your ceiling until you have explored the continent's edges.

### Guide must account for
- A continent survey prioritized early, since it determines the size of the whole game.
- An explicit prohibition on overseas settling, including tempting captured cities.
- A pivot for small continents: a faster victory path instead of a larger empire.
