# Lautaro — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Mapuche  |  **Leader Title:** Toqui of the Mapuche
**Source:** Rise and Fall

**Leader Ability — Swift Hawk**
Defeating an enemy unit in enemy territory reduces the Loyalty of that unit's origin city, and Lautaro's units get a combat bonus when fighting civilizations that are in a Golden or Heroic Age, letting him punish and destabilize thriving rivals through raiding.

**Agenda — Spirit of Tucapel**
Lautaro works to keep Loyalty high in his own cities and dislikes other civilizations that let their own cities' Loyalty slip, favoring stable, well-governed rivals.

**Civilization Ability — Toqui**
Cities with an established Governor gain bonus Culture, Production, and combat experience for trained units, and radiate Loyalty pressure toward nearby Mapuche cities, letting the Mapuche use Governors to both develop and hold their territory against Loyalty pressure.

**Unique Units**
- Malón Raider (replaces none (unique Renaissance-era light cavalry)) — A hard-hitting raiding cavalry unit that gains a combat bonus near friendly territory and can pillage for a single movement point, ideal for hit-and-run war-of-attrition strategies that punish rival Loyalty.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Chemamull (Improvement) — A high-Appeal-requirement tile improvement that converts Appeal into Culture (and Tourism after Flight), making it a strong late-game culture/tourism engine for high-Appeal Mapuche territory.

### Historical Background
Lautaro was a young Mapuche man captured and pressed into service by Spanish conquistador Pedro de Valdivia in 16th-century Chile, during which time he learned Spanish cavalry and siege tactics from the inside. He escaped and used that knowledge to lead Mapuche forces in the Arauco War, most famously defeating and killing Valdivia at the Battle of Tucapel in 1553. His guerrilla-style raiding tactics and refusal to hold static ground helped the Mapuche resist full Spanish conquest for centuries, longer than almost any other indigenous American people, a legacy echoed in his in-game focus on mobile raiding and loyalty-destabilizing warfare against more 'settled' golden-age rivals.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Monitor
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Conditional | **Condition cost:** Depends entirely on other civs entering Golden Ages

> The core loop is a stat check, not a build order. These guides fail hardest when written as sequences, because the player's real job is watching one number and responding to it.

### Setup
*Turns: 1 to roughly 25*

Establish the thing being monitored and the infrastructure that lets you respond to it.

- Name the watched stat on turn 1: Rival era score / Golden Age status (external trigger)
- Establish its bands before it matters, so the player recognizes trouble the first time rather than the third.
- Build the response capacity. A monitor with no available response is just anxiety.

### Standing Loop
*Turns: 25 to end of game*

This replaces the turn list entirely. The guide is this loop plus the era checkpoints.

- Read Rival era score / Golden Age status (external trigger).
- Green band: continue current build. No action.
- Amber band: begin the correction now. Amber is the acting band; red is the failing band.
- Red band: the ability is off or about to be. Everything else is deprioritized until it clears.

### Era Checkpoints
*Turns: each era boundary*

Re-read the role. Monitor leaders change what the stat means across eras.

- Ancient/Classical: Dormant. No Golden Ages exist to punish yet.
- Medieval/Renaissance: Opportunistic spoiler against whoever is ahead.
- Industrial/Modern: Same role, dependent on rival era scores.
- Atomic/Information: Same role, increasingly situational.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Watched stat sits in the red band for more than roughly 15 turns with no recovery path.
- Stop paying the Depends entirely on other civs entering Golden Ages. If the bonus is unreachable, its price is pure loss.
- Rebuild as a generic civ around whatever the monitored strategy already produced.

### Fail state
The stat crossed into red and stayed. For Lautaro, The trigger belongs to other players. There are eras where the ability simply does not apply.

### Caveats
- The trigger belongs to other players. There are eras where the ability simply does not apply.
- Loyalty flipping requires population pressure to already be in place; the kills alone do not do it.
- Verify the direction of the ability before writing the guide: the loyalty pressure keys off kills in his own territory.

### Guide must account for
- Rival era score as a monitored per-turn stat, since it determines whether the kit is live.
- A default plan for eras where no rival is in a Golden Age.
- Loyalty pressure groundwork built before the opportunity appears, not in response to it.
