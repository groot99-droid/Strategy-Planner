# Hojo Tokimune — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Japanese  |  **Leader Title:** Regent of the Kamakura Shogunate
**Source:** Base Game

**Leader Ability — Divine Wind**
Japanese land units get +5 Combat Strength on land tiles adjacent to Coast, and naval units get +5 Combat Strength in shallow water; Encampment, Holy Site, and Theater Square districts are also built in half the normal time, making Japan strong at coastal warfare and fast at fielding military/cultural/religious infrastructure.

**Agenda — Bushido**
Tokimune favors civilizations that pair a strong military with high Faith and Culture output, and disapproves of civilizations that are militarily powerful but weak in Faith and Culture.

**Civilization Ability — Meiji Restoration**
Japanese districts receive the full (standard) adjacency bonus from being placed next to other districts, instead of the reduced 'minor' bonus other civilizations get, letting Japan pack districts tightly together for outsized yields.

**Unique Units**
- Samurai (replaces Man-At-Arms) — A stronger Medieval-era melee unit that suffers no combat penalty when damaged, letting it keep fighting effectively even at low health.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Electronics Factory (Building) — Replaces the Factory; a late-game production building that keeps Japan's tightly-packed district cities industrially competitive.

### Historical Background
Hojo Tokimune served as regent (shikken) of Japan's Kamakura shogunate from 1268 to 1284, a period defined by the Mongol Empire's two attempted invasions of Japan in 1274 and 1281. Tokimune organized Japan's samurai defenses and coastal fortifications to repel both invasions, and the second fleet was famously destroyed by a typhoon the Japanese called 'kamikaze,' or 'divine wind' -- the direct namesake of his in-game leader ability. A devout patron of Zen Buddhism, he is remembered as a symbol of samurai resolve and Japan's successful defense of its coastline, which the ability's coastal combat bonuses and fast military infrastructure evoke directly.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Front-Load
**Curve:** Flat | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Tight city spacing; rewards planning discipline

> Nearly every decision that matters is made in the first twenty turns and cannot be reversed. Guide density should be inverted: dense at the start, sparse after.

### Turn 0 — Assessment
*Turns: before the first Settler moves*

A genuine go or no-go on the start position. This phase exists only in this shape and it is the most valuable thing in the template.

- Check the hard requirement: Tight city spacing; rewards planning discipline
- If the requirement is absent within a reasonable walk, decide now whether to move the Settler or play the leader as a generic civ. Deciding this at turn 40 is deciding it too late.
- Estimate the ceiling. For radius- and continent-capped leaders, the ceiling is knowable early and determines the victory path.

### Turns 1-20 — Commit
*Turns: 1 to 20*

Make every irreversible decision deliberately rather than incidentally.

- Draw the full settle map before the second Settler is produced, including sites you will not reach for 40 turns.
- Assign district roles to each city site at settle time, not at build time. For placement-restricted leaders this is the whole game.
- Mark each decision with its reversibility. The guide should say plainly which choices are permanent.
- A city spacing plan drawn before the second Settler moves.

### Turns 20-80 — Execute
*Turns: 20 to 80*

Build out the plan drawn in the commit phase. Low decision density by design.

- Follow the settle map. Deviation is the common failure, and it usually feels reasonable at the time.
- Peak. Compact high-yield complexes across every city.

### Turn 80+ — Live With It
*Turns: 80 onward*

Operate inside the ceiling set at turn 20.

- Choose a victory path that fits the ceiling rather than fighting it.
- Still strong, though spacing begins to cap growth.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Turn 0 assessment fails and no acceptable alternative start is within reach.
- Play the leader as a generic civ and say so explicitly. A named fallback is better than a strategy that quietly cannot work.
- Target a faster victory, since the capped ceiling will not win a long game.

### Fail state
A placement decision made before turn 20 has capped the game, and the cap will not be visible until roughly turn 100. Tight spacing that helps adjacency also caps late-game population per city.

### Caveats
- Tight spacing that helps adjacency also caps late-game population per city.
- The half-cost applies to three district types only; everything else is normal price.
- Adjacency requires deliberate placement order, so build sequence is a real decision.

### Guide must account for
- A city spacing plan drawn before the second Settler moves.
- District placement order as an explicit sequence, since later placements depend on earlier ones.
- The tradeoff point where tighter spacing starts costing more than the adjacency returns.
