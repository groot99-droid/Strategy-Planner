# Pericles — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Greece  |  **Leader Title:** First Citizen of Athens
**Source:** Base Game

**Leader Ability — Surrounded by Glory**
Pericles gains bonus Culture for every city-state of which he is Suzerain, rewarding an aggressive envoy/diplomacy strategy aimed at accumulating city-state alliances.

**Agenda — Delian League**
Pericles favors leaders who are not competing with him for the same city-states' suzerainty, and dislikes leaders who directly contest the city-state alliances he is pursuing.

**Civilization Ability — Plato's Republic**
Greece gains an extra Wildcard policy card slot in every government, giving it unusual flexibility to mix and match policy cards for whichever strategy (culture, science, or otherwise) it is pursuing.

**Unique Units**
- Hoplite (replaces Spearman) — Gains a very large Combat Strength bonus when adjacent to another Hoplite, rewarding tight phalanx-style formations reminiscent of Greek warfare.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Acropolis (District) — Replaces the Theater Square, must be built on a Hill, and gets stronger Culture adjacency bonuses from neighboring districts (including the City Center), making Greek cities strong culture generators.

### Historical Background
Pericles (c. 495-429 BCE) was the leading statesman of Athens during its Golden Age, guiding the city through the height of its democratic institutions, its cultural flourishing (including the Parthenon and other Acropolis monuments, echoed by Greece's unique district), and the early years of the Peloponnesian War against Sparta. He was famously eulogized by Thucydides as effectively Athens' 'first citizen' despite holding no monarchical title, a nod reflected in his in-game leader title. His policy of forging and managing an alliance network among Greek city-states — the Delian League — is the direct inspiration for his in-game agenda's focus on city-state suzerainty.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Monitor
**Curve:** Flat | **Conversion:** Conditional | **Condition cost:** Ongoing envoy investment; suzerainty can be taken from you

> The core loop is a stat check, not a build order. These guides fail hardest when written as sequences, because the player's real job is watching one number and responding to it.

### Setup
*Turns: 1 to roughly 25*

Establish the thing being monitored and the infrastructure that lets you respond to it.

- Name the watched stat on turn 1: Suzerainty count and contested city-state envoy gaps
- Establish its bands before it matters, so the player recognizes trouble the first time rather than the third.
- Build the response capacity. A monitor with no available response is just anxiety.

### Standing Loop
*Turns: 25 to end of game*

This replaces the turn list entirely. The guide is this loop plus the era checkpoints.

- Read Suzerainty count and contested city-state envoy gaps.
- Green band: continue current build. No action.
- Amber band: begin the correction now. Amber is the acting band; red is the failing band.
- Red band: the ability is off or about to be. Everything else is deprioritized until it clears.

### Era Checkpoints
*Turns: each era boundary*

Re-read the role. Monitor leaders change what the stat means across eras.

- Ancient/Classical: Envoy accumulation and early culture districts.
- Medieval/Renaissance: Multiplier at full effect across a growing culture base.
- Industrial/Modern: Strong, still contingent on holding suzerainties.
- Atomic/Information: Culture victory position, still rented.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Watched stat sits in the red band for more than roughly 15 turns with no recovery path.
- Stop paying the Ongoing envoy investment; suzerainty can be taken from you. If the bonus is unreachable, its price is pure loss.
- Rebuild as a generic civ around whatever the monitored strategy already produced.

### Fail state
The stat crossed into red and stayed. For Pericles, Suzerainty can be outbid at any time, and the culture loss is immediate and global.

### Caveats
- Suzerainty can be outbid at any time, and the culture loss is immediate and global.
- A percentage of nothing is nothing. The multiplier needs real culture districts underneath it.
- Envoy competition intensifies as other civs pursue diplomatic victory.

### Guide must account for
- An envoy budget per era, plus a defense reserve for contested city-states.
- Culture district construction as a parallel mandatory track, not an optional one.
- Suzerainty count as a monitored stat with a response plan when one is lost.
