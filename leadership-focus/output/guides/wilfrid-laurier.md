# Wilfrid Laurier — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Canada  |  **Leader Title:** Prime Minister of Canada
**Source:** Gathering Storm

**Leader Ability — The Last Best West**
Canada can build Farms on Tundra tiles from the start of the game (and on Tundra Hills after Engineering), Mines/Lumber Mills on Tundra and Snow gain bonus Production, and Camps/Farms on those tiles gain bonus Food; flat Tundra/Snow and Tundra/Snow Hills tiles can also be purchased for less Gold, turning otherwise weak cold terrain into productive land.

**Agenda — Canadian Expeditionary Force**
Laurier responds to World Congress Emergencies whenever possible and dislikes leaders who refuse to participate in Emergencies.

**Civilization Ability — Four Faces of Peace**
Canada cannot declare or be the target of Surprise Wars, and cannot declare war on city-states; it also gains Diplomatic Favor for accumulated Tourism and for completing Emergencies and Scored Competitions, making Canada a strongly diplomacy- and peace-oriented civilization.

**Unique Units**
- Mountie (replaces none (standalone Modern era light cavalry unit)) — A low-maintenance cavalry unit that can found a National Park and gains a large Combat Strength bonus near National Parks (even more if Canadian-owned), primarily useful for defense rather than conquest.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Ice Hockey Rink (Improvement) — Built on Tundra or Snow tiles, it grants Appeal, Amenities, Culture, and other bonuses to adjacent cold terrain, complementing Canada's tundra-focused kit.

### Historical Background
Sir Wilfrid Laurier (1841-1919) served as Canada's seventh Prime Minister from 1896 to 1911, presiding over a period of major western settlement and economic growth often summarized by his own phrase that the 20th century would 'belong to Canada.' He championed a policy of national unity between English and French Canadians and pursued a cautious, peace-oriented approach to Canada's relationship with the British Empire, reflected in the in-game civilization's inability to fight surprise wars or attack city-states. His government actively promoted immigration and settlement of the Canadian prairies and north, directly inspiring 'The Last Best West' ability's focus on making Tundra land productive.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Dead-Phase
**Curve:** Bloom | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Tundra start; the terrain bonus is survival, not growth

> The hardest template to write and the most necessary. The leader contributes nothing for 80 to 120 turns. Without an explicit early game AND an explicit statement that the flatness is intended, players abandon the plan around turn 60.

### Phase A — Dormant
*Turns: 1 to roughly 150*

Survive, develop, and quietly accumulate the prerequisite the payoff phase consumes.

- Run a complete generic build order. The guide must supply this in full, because the leader supplies no direction.
- Maintain a defensive floor. Elimination during Phase A is the single most common way these leaders lose, and it is invisible in the ability description.
- Accumulate the prerequisite even though it produces nothing yet: Preserve civic unlocked and tundra appeal groundwork laid for future National Parks
- Provide non-ability progress markers so the player has feedback: city count, science rate, district count. Something must be visibly improving.

### Phase B — Activation
*Turns: roughly 150*

The kit switches on. Verify the accumulated prerequisite is actually sufficient.

- Check the stockpile. Activation with an empty stockpile wastes the entire dormant phase.
- Peak. National Parks and tourism spike.
- Reorient production toward the payoff. Phase A build habits are now wrong.

### Phase C — Payoff
*Turns: 150 onward*

Convert the accumulated position at speed. The remaining game is short, so this phase runs hot.

- Strong culture position.
- Push the victory condition directly. There is no time for a second build-out.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Reaching activation with the prerequisite stockpile empty, or having lost cities during the dormant phase.
- Accept a lesser victory path built on the generic empire rather than the leader ability.
- The leader ability is now decorative. Say so and stop planning around it.

### Fail state
Crippled or eliminated before activation, or arriving at activation with nothing stockpiled. Both are Phase A failures that only become visible in Phase B.

### Caveats
- Surprise War immunity is not immunity to formal war. A prepared aggressor can still declare.
- Tundra means low food and production, so everything takes longer than the guide's default pacing.
- The late tourism spike needs Parks, which need appeal and Preserves in place well beforehand.

### Guide must account for
- A stated low-output expectation for the first two eras so pacing feels intentional.
- A formal war defense plan, since the immunity covers only one war type.
- National Park groundwork begun long before the era where it pays.
