# Philip II — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Spain  |  **Leader Title:** none
**Source:** Base Game

**Leader Ability — El Escorial**
Philip's Inquisitors gain an extra use of Remove Heresy, and his military and religious units gain a Combat or Religious Strength bonus against units belonging to civilizations that follow a different religion, rewarding religious uniformity backed by force.

**Agenda — Counter Reformer**
Philip II wants every one of his cities to follow the same religion he does, favors civilizations that share his faith, and strongly dislikes anyone who spreads a different religion into his empire.

**Civilization Ability — Treasure Fleet**
Spain gains bonus yields from intercontinental Trade Routes and can form naval units into Fleets and Armadas earlier than other civilizations, reflecting Spain's historical dominance of transoceanic trade.

**Unique Units**
- Conquistador — A Renaissance-era unit that gains a large Combat Strength bonus when escorted by a religious unit and automatically converts captured cities to Spain's religion, combining conquest with religious conversion.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Mission (Improvement) — A tile improvement providing Faith and Science, with extra yields when built adjacent to a Campus or on a continent other than Spain's home continent, rewarding overseas expansion.

### Historical Background
Philip II of Spain (reigned 1556-1598) ruled the largest empire the world had yet seen, spanning territories in Europe, the Americas, Africa, and Asia, funded in large part by silver shipped home in fleets from the New World. A fiercely devout Catholic monarch, he positioned himself as Europe's foremost defender of the Counter-Reformation, waging war against Protestant England and the Dutch rebels and overseeing the Spanish Inquisition's campaign against heresy. His combination of a religiously enforced empire and treasure-fleet-driven wealth is reflected directly in Spain's civilization ability and Philip's religious-conformity agenda and Inquisitor bonus.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Gate
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Conditional | **Condition cost:** Bonus only applies against differently-faithed civs

> The strategy does not begin on a turn. It begins on an unlock. This needs two complete guides with a trigger between them, not one guide with a note in the middle.

### Pre-Gate
*Turns: 1 until the gate opens*

Survive, develop, and stage. This is a real phase with its own complete build order, not a waiting room.

- State the gate explicitly on turn 1 so the player knows what they are working toward: Religion founded, Renaissance era reached (for Conquistadors)
- Give an estimated arrival turn AND the research or civic path that gets there fastest.
- Build the prerequisites the gate assumes but does not provide. Bonus only applies against differently-faithed civs
- Maintain a defensive floor. The gate does not protect you while you wait for it.

### Gate Check
*Turns: variable*

A single decision point, not a phase.


### Post-Gate
*Turns: gate onward*

The actual leader strategy, now live.

- Peak. Conquistadors against differently-faithed neighbors.
- Run the closing-window check: most Gate abilities are also time-limited on the far end. Identify when parity arrives.
- Convert before parity. Conditional

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Gate opens with staging incomplete and a rival is already at parity.
- Do not open the strategy. Playing an unstaged Gate leader into a prepared opponent is the worst outcome available.
- Fold into a generic plan built on whatever the pre-gate phase produced.

### Fail state
The gate opened, the window closed, and the strategy never ran. The pre-gate phase was treated as filler and produced nothing usable.

### Caveats
- If a target adopts your religion, your combat bonus against them vanishes entirely.
- The unit bonus requires a religious unit stacked with the military unit, which is a logistics burden.
- The Conquistador window is narrow and era-bound.

### Guide must account for
- A faith check on each target run before declaring, since it determines whether the kit applies.
- Paired military and religious units as a standard formation in the build plan.
- A caution against spreading your own religion to intended targets.
