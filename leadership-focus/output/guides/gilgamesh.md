# Gilgamesh — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Sumeria  |  **Leader Title:** King of Uruk
**Source:** Base Game

**Leader Ability — Adventures of Enkidu**
When Gilgamesh declares war on a civilization that is already at war with one of his Allies, he suffers no warmonger penalty for it, and his units share pillage rewards and combat experience with the nearest allied unit within a few tiles. This rewards Gilgamesh for piling onto wars his allies have already started rather than starting his own.

**Agenda — Ally of Enkidu**
Gilgamesh wants to form long-term Alliances and likes leaders willing to ally with him. He dislikes anyone who denounces or attacks his friends and allies.

**Civilization Ability — Epic Quest**
Sumeria receives a free Tribal Village-style reward each time it captures a Barbarian Outpost, and the Gold cost to levy military units from city-states is halved, making early aggressive expansion and city-state levying unusually cheap.

**Unique Units**
- War-Cart (replaces Chariot Archer) — An early ranged unit that hits harder than the standard Chariot Archer, letting Sumeria punch above its weight in the Ancient era.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Ziggurat (Improvement) — A unique tile improvement that boosts Science and Faith, rewarding Sumeria for building tall with strong early yields outside of districts.

### Historical Background
Gilgamesh was the legendary king of the Sumerian city-state of Uruk, likely based on a real ruler from around 2700 BCE, and is the hero of the Epic of Gilgamesh, one of the oldest surviving works of literature. The epic recounts his friendship with the wild man Enkidu, their shared adventures and battles against monsters, and Gilgamesh's grief-driven quest for immortality after Enkidu's death. That central friendship is directly reflected in his Civ VI leader ability, Adventures of Enkidu, which rewards fighting alongside allies rather than alone. Uruk itself was one of the world's first true cities, and Gilgamesh was famed for building its great walls.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Countdown
**Curve:** Spike | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** None
**Convert by:** turn 80

> The ability has an expiry. The guide is a timer wrapped around a conversion deadline. Its most important content is not the build order, it is the turn the plan stops and what replaces it.

### Phase 1 — Arm
*Turns: 1 to roughly 30*

Reach the window with everything already staged. Nothing in this phase is optional; staging done after the window opens is spent from the budget.

- Turn 1: identify the target. Gilgamesh's window is short enough that target selection cannot wait for scouting to finish.
- Turns 1-15: scout toward the target only. Exploration away from the target is a luxury this shape cannot afford.
- Turns 10-30: production goes to the units or charges the window will consume. Infrastructure that pays back after 80 is deferred.
- Pre-window checkpoint: confirm None is satisfied and units are within striking distance, not at home.

### Phase 2 — Window
*Turns: roughly 30 to 80*

Convert tempo into permanent assets. Cities, wonders, or improvements. Not kills, not gold, not map presence.


**Checkpoints:**
- *midpoint of window*: Is at least half the objective secured? → if yes: proceed; if no: Reduce scope now. Take one city instead of three. Partial conversion beats total expiry.
- *80*: Has the tempo become something permanent? → if yes: Proceed to Phase 3.; if no: Abort branch.

### Phase 3 — Digest
*Turns: 80 onward*

Stabilize what was taken. This phase is longer than both prior phases combined and most guides for this shape underwrite it badly.

- Amenity and loyalty audit on every acquired city before any new construction.
- Infrastructure catch-up: the arming phase deliberately skipped this, and the bill comes due now.
- Choose a victory path. Gilgamesh has no leader-specific direction from here; Generic wide empire. Leader ability contributes nothing.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Window closed with no permanent gain, or three stalled turns before the midpoint.
- Make peace immediately. Prolonging a failed window converts a bad position into a lost game.
- Disband surplus units. The maintenance is now pure loss.
- Switch to the generic development plan for the empire you actually have.

### Fail state
The window closed and nothing permanent was gained. Gilgamesh is now an ordinary civ with an army it cannot afford and grievances it cannot repay.

### Caveats
- War-Carts obsolete and their upgrade path is expensive; the unit advantage is a window, not a standing army.
- Captured cities arrive with no infrastructure and immediately tax amenities and loyalty.
- The barbarian outpost reward encourages leaving camps alive, which is also how you get raided.

### Guide must account for
- A hard stop-conquering turn, after which the guide switches entirely to consolidation.
- An amenity and loyalty checkpoint after every single capture, not at fixed intervals.
- An abort condition: if the first neighbor is not taken by the deadline, what replaces the plan.
