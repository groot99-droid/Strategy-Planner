# Ba Trieu — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Vietnamese  |  **Leader Title:** Lady Trieu, folk heroine of Vietnamese resistance
**Source:** New Frontier Pass

**Leader Ability — Drive Out The Aggressors**
Vietnamese units gain bonus Movement and Combat Strength while on Woods, Rainforest, or Marsh tiles, with the bonus doubled when those tiles are inside Vietnam's own territory, making Vietnam extremely strong at defending its jungle terrain against invasion.

**Agenda — Defender of the Homeland**
Ba Trieu favors civilizations that have never declared war on Vietnam, and permanently distrusts and dislikes any civilization that ever has, regardless of how much time has passed.

**Civilization Ability — Nine Dragon River Delta**
Vietnam can only place specialty land districts on Woods, Rainforest, or Marsh tiles, but buildings in those districts gain bonus Culture, Science, or Production depending on the terrain feature, turning a placement restriction into a themed yield bonus.

**Unique Units**
- Voi Chiến (replaces Crossbowman) — A Medieval ranged unit with more Movement and Sight than the Crossbowman, and the ability to move after attacking, well suited to Vietnam's mobile jungle-terrain warfare.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Thành (District) — Replaces the Encampment; provides bonus Culture for each adjacent district and, once Flight is researched, converts that Culture output into Tourism, giving Vietnam a militarized district with a late-game culture payoff.

### Historical Background
Triệu Thị Trinh, known as Bà Triệu ('Lady Trieu'), was a 3rd-century CE Vietnamese woman who led an armed rebellion against occupying Chinese (Eastern Wu) rule around 248 CE. Vietnamese tradition holds she declared she would rather 'ride the wind and walk the waves' and fight as a warrior than live bowed under foreign rule as a servant, and though her rebellion was ultimately crushed, she became -- alongside the earlier Trung Sisters -- one of Vietnam's most enduring symbols of resistance to foreign domination. That legacy of guerrilla resistance fought from Vietnam's dense terrain against a more powerful invader directly informs her in-game kit, which rewards fighting from (and defending) Vietnam's woods, rainforest, and marsh tiles and never forgives a civilization that has declared war on her.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Front-Load
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Districts only on Woods, Rainforest, or Marsh. Severe placement restriction.

> Nearly every decision that matters is made in the first twenty turns and cannot be reversed. Guide density should be inverted: dense at the start, sparse after.

### Turn 0 — Assessment
*Turns: before the first Settler moves*

A genuine go or no-go on the start position. This phase exists only in this shape and it is the most valuable thing in the template.

- Check the hard requirement: Districts only on Woods, Rainforest, or Marsh. Severe placement restriction.
- If the requirement is absent within a reasonable walk, decide now whether to move the Settler or play the leader as a generic civ. Deciding this at turn 40 is deciding it too late.
- Estimate the ceiling. For radius- and continent-capped leaders, the ceiling is knowable early and determines the victory path.

### Turns 1-20 — Commit
*Turns: 1 to 20*

Make every irreversible decision deliberately rather than incidentally.

- Draw the full settle map before the second Settler is produced, including sites you will not reach for 40 turns.
- Assign district roles to each city site at settle time, not at build time. For placement-restricted leaders this is the whole game.
- Mark each decision with its reversibility. The guide should say plainly which choices are permanent.
- A terrain audit run before every single settle, not just the first.

### Turns 20-80 — Execute
*Turns: 20 to 80*

Build out the plan drawn in the commit phase. Low decision density by design.

- Follow the settle map. Deviation is the common failure, and it usually feels reasonable at the time.
- Defensive fortress with strong terrain-based culture.

### Turn 80+ — Live With It
*Turns: 80 onward*

Operate inside the ceiling set at turn 20.

- Choose a victory path that fits the ceiling rather than fighting it.
- Still defensively excellent, offensively limited.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Turn 0 assessment fails and no acceptable alternative start is within reach.
- Play the leader as a generic civ and say so explicitly. A named fallback is better than a strategy that quietly cannot work.
- Target a faster victory, since the capped ceiling will not win a long game.

### Fail state
A placement decision made before turn 20 has capped the game, and the cap will not be visible until roughly turn 100. The district restriction is permanent and can make necessary districts genuinely unbuildable in some cities.

### Caveats
- The district restriction is permanent and can make necessary districts genuinely unbuildable in some cities.
- Some starts are close to unplayable without waiting on the civic that allows planting Woods.
- Every settle decision is also a district decision, made turns before the district exists.

### Guide must account for
- A terrain audit run before every single settle, not just the first.
- District planning resolved at settle time rather than build time.
- A recovery path for cities that end up unable to host a needed district.
