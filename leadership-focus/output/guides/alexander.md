# Alexander — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Macedon  |  **Leader Title:** The Great
**Source:** Rise and Fall

**Leader Ability — To the World's End**
Removes war weariness in Alexander's cities, heals his units when he captures a city containing a Wonder, and unlocks his unique Hetairoi heavy cavalry unit, supporting a strategy of continuous, far-flung conquest without the usual unhappiness penalties.

**Agenda — Short Life of Glory**
Alexander favors civilizations that are at war with someone other than himself and dislikes those at peace; in Gathering Storm, Grievances against him also decay twice as fast as normal.

**Civilization Ability — Hellenistic Fusion**
When Macedon captures a foreign city, it gains a bonus tied to the types of Districts already present there, letting Alexander's empire absorb and benefit from the specializations of conquered territory rather than razing them.

**Unique Units**
- Hetairoi (replaces Horseman) — A powerful heavy cavalry unit unlocked by Alexander's leader ability, giving Macedon an early mounted strike force well ahead of other civilizations.
- Hypaspist (replaces Swordsman) — Gains a combat bonus when attacking districts and cities, making Macedon especially effective at siege and city capture.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Basilikoi Paides (Building) — Boosts Production, Housing, and combat experience for units trained in the city, and grants Science based on the cost of units produced, tying Macedon's military machine to both growth and research.

### Historical Background
Alexander the Great became king of Macedon in 336 BCE and, within thirteen years, conquered the entire Persian Empire, extending Macedonian rule from Greece to Egypt and northwest India before his death at age 32 in 323 BCE. His conquests spread Greek (Hellenistic) language, art, and institutions across the ancient world, blending with local cultures in the regions he took — the direct inspiration for Macedon's civ ability, which lets the empire absorb bonuses from captured cities rather than simply destroying them. After his death his generals, the Diadochi, carved his empire into rival successor kingdoms, and his relentless campaigning without ever losing a battle is echoed by his agenda's restless preference for a civilization always at war.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Monitor
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Conditional | **Condition cost:** Must stay at war to keep the engine running

> The core loop is a stat check, not a build order. These guides fail hardest when written as sequences, because the player's real job is watching one number and responding to it.

### Setup
*Turns: 1 to roughly 25*

Establish the thing being monitored and the infrastructure that lets you respond to it.

- Name the watched stat on turn 1: Current war/peace state and next-target readiness
- Establish its bands before it matters, so the player recognizes trouble the first time rather than the third.
- Build the response capacity. A monitor with no available response is just anxiety.

### Standing Loop
*Turns: 25 to end of game*

This replaces the turn list entirely. The guide is this loop plus the era checkpoints.

- Read Current war/peace state and next-target readiness.
- Green band: continue current build. No action.
- Amber band: begin the correction now. Amber is the acting band; red is the failing band.
- Red band: the ability is off or about to be. Everything else is deprioritized until it clears.

### Era Checkpoints
*Turns: each era boundary*

Re-read the role. Monitor leaders change what the stat means across eras.

- Ancient/Classical: Encampment build-out and first wars.
- Medieval/Renaissance: Peak. Continuous war with no domestic penalty.
- Industrial/Modern: Still warring, but tech parity becomes the binding constraint.
- Atomic/Information: Functional, no longer special.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Watched stat sits in the red band for more than roughly 15 turns with no recovery path.
- Stop paying the Must stay at war to keep the engine running. If the bonus is unreachable, its price is pure loss.
- Rebuild as a generic civ around whatever the monitored strategy already produced.

### Fail state
The stat crossed into red and stayed. For Alexander, Peace switches the kit off. There is no reward for consolidation phases.

### Caveats
- Peace switches the kit off. There is no reward for consolidation phases.
- No war weariness is not no unhappiness. Loyalty and amenities in captured cities still bite.
- Science from unit training only pays while you keep training. Stopping stops the science.

### Guide must account for
- A rolling target queue with the next war identified before the current one ends.
- Loyalty, not weariness, as the monitored stat during occupation.
- A continuous unit production line treated as a science building, not a military decision.
