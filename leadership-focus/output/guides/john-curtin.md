# John Curtin — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Australian  |  **Leader Title:** Prime Minister of Australia
**Source:** Rise and Fall (Australia Civilization & Scenario Pack, a standalone DLC released alongside Rise and Fall, not part of New Frontier Pass)

**Leader Ability — Citadel of Civilization**
Australia gains a large temporary Production bonus for a set number of turns after being on the receiving end of a Declaration of War, and an even longer-lasting Production bonus after liberating a captured city, letting Curtin turn being attacked (or liberating others) into a Production surge.

**Agenda — Perpetually on Guard**
Curtin seeks Defensive Pacts with other civilizations and likes leaders who liberate captured cities, while disliking those who occupy and hold cities they've conquered.

**Civilization Ability — Land Down Under**
Coastal cities get bonus Housing, Pastures can trigger a Culture Bomb to claim surrounding territory, and Campus, Commercial Hub, Holy Site, and Theater Square districts get bonus yields on tiles with high Appeal, rewarding Australia for building scenic, coastal, well-developed cities.

**Unique Units**
- Digger (replaces Infantry) — A stronger Modern-era infantry unit with combat bonuses on land tiles adjacent to water and when fighting outside Australian territory, fitting an expeditionary, coastal-defense playstyle.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Outback Station (Improvement) — A unique tile improvement unlocked with Guilds (and upgradeable later) that develops otherwise marginal outback terrain, supporting Australia's wide, resource-heavy territory.

### Historical Background
John Curtin served as Australia's Prime Minister from 1941 to 1945, leading the country through the most direct existential threat it had faced: the Japanese advance through the Pacific during World War II, including air raids on Darwin and the fall of Singapore. Facing the limits of British support, Curtin controversially redirected Australian troops home from the Middle East and pivoted Australia's strategic alliance toward the United States, reshaping Australian foreign policy for the rest of the century. He died in office in July 1945, shortly before the war's end, worn down by the strain of wartime leadership. His defining legacy of turning a defensive crisis into national mobilization and a lasting policy of forming strong external alliances is reflected directly in his in-game kit's war-triggered production bonuses and preference for Defensive Pacts.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Monitor (secondary: Linear)
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Expiring | **Condition cost:** Requires being attacked, which you do not control

> The core loop is a stat check, not a build order. These guides fail hardest when written as sequences, because the player's real job is watching one number and responding to it.

### Alt-shape note
The production-burst response is a reactive stat-check and IS his caveats.json primary shape (Monitor). Baseline empire management underneath it is ordinary era-block play.

**Render rule:** Render the Monitor template as the spine (already primary). Insert template.Linear.structure era-block phases as the 'default state' content beneath each Monitor checkpoint, so the guide reads as era blocks with a standing watch layered on top rather than a bare stat-check with no baseline plan.

### Setup
*Turns: 1 to roughly 25*

Establish the thing being monitored and the infrastructure that lets you respond to it.

- Name the watched stat on turn 1: War/threat status (are we currently being attacked)
- Establish its bands before it matters, so the player recognizes trouble the first time rather than the third.
- Build the response capacity. A monitor with no available response is just anxiety.

### Standing Loop
*Turns: 25 to end of game*

This replaces the turn list entirely. The guide is this loop plus the era checkpoints.

- Read War/threat status (are we currently being attacked).
- Green band: continue current build. No action.
- Amber band: begin the correction now. Amber is the acting band; red is the failing band.
- Red band: the ability is off or about to be. Everything else is deprioritized until it clears.

### Era Checkpoints
*Turns: each era boundary*

Re-read the role. Monitor leaders change what the stat means across eras.

- Ancient/Classical: Passive. Appeal-aware settling with no active ability use.
- Medieval/Renaissance: Baiting aggression to trigger production bursts.
- Industrial/Modern: Peak. Bursts dumped into high-cost projects.
- Atomic/Information: Space race acceleration.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Watched stat sits in the red band for more than roughly 15 turns with no recovery path.
- Stop paying the Requires being attacked, which you do not control. If the bonus is unreachable, its price is pure loss.
- Rebuild as a generic civ around whatever the monitored strategy already produced.

### Fail state
The stat crossed into red and stayed. For John Curtin, The trigger requires being attacked, which you do not control and cannot schedule.

### Caveats
- The trigger requires being attacked, which you do not control and cannot schedule.
- Baiting aggression risks actually losing cities to a competent aggressor.
- A burst with nothing queued is a burst wasted; the ten turns do not wait.

### Guide must account for
- A pre-queued list of expensive builds kept ready at all times to absorb a burst.
- A defensive floor that keeps baiting from becoming actual defeat.
- A fallback plan for a game where nobody ever attacks.
