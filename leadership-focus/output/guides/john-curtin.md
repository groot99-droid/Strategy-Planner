# John Curtin — Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Monitor (secondary: Linear)
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Expiring | **Condition cost:** Requires being attacked, which you do not control

> The core loop is a stat check, not a build order. These guides fail hardest when written as sequences, because the player's real job is watching one number and responding to it.

## Setup
*Turns: 1 to roughly 25*

Establish the thing being monitored and the infrastructure that lets you respond to it.

- Name the watched stat on turn 1: War/threat status (are we currently being attacked)
- Establish its bands before it matters, so the player recognizes trouble the first time rather than the third.
- Build the response capacity. A monitor with no available response is just anxiety.

## Standing Loop
*Turns: 25 to end of game*

This replaces the turn list entirely. The guide is this loop plus the era checkpoints.

- Read War/threat status (are we currently being attacked).
- Green band: continue current build. No action.
- Amber band: begin the correction now. Amber is the acting band; red is the failing band.
- Red band: the ability is off or about to be. Everything else is deprioritized until it clears.

## Era Checkpoints
*Turns: each era boundary*

Re-read the role. Monitor leaders change what the stat means across eras.

- Ancient/Classical: Passive. Appeal-aware settling with no active ability use.
- Medieval/Renaissance: Baiting aggression to trigger production bursts.
- Industrial/Modern: Peak. Bursts dumped into high-cost projects.
- Atomic/Information: Space race acceleration.

## Abort branch
**Trigger:** Watched stat sits in the red band for more than roughly 15 turns with no recovery path.
- Stop paying the Requires being attacked, which you do not control. If the bonus is unreachable, its price is pure loss.
- Rebuild as a generic civ around whatever the monitored strategy already produced.

## Fail state
The stat crossed into red and stayed. For John Curtin, The trigger requires being attacked, which you do not control and cannot schedule.

## Caveats
- The trigger requires being attacked, which you do not control and cannot schedule.
- Baiting aggression risks actually losing cities to a competent aggressor.
- A burst with nothing queued is a burst wasted; the ten turns do not wait.

## Guide must account for
- A pre-queued list of expensive builds kept ready at all times to absorb a burst.
- A defensive floor that keeps baiting from becoming actual defeat.
- A fallback plan for a game where nobody ever attacks.
