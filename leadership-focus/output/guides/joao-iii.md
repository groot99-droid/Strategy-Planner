# Joao III — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Portugal  |  **Leader Title:** none
**Source:** New Frontier Pass

**Leader Ability — Porta do Cerco**
All of João's units gain extra Sight, meeting a new civilization grants an additional Trade Route capacity, and Portugal automatically has Open Borders with all city-states, supporting an exploration-and-trade-focused playstyle from the earliest turns.

**Agenda — Navigator's Legacy**
João III loves exploring the map and favors other civilizations that prioritize exploration as well.

**Civilization Ability — Casa da Índia**
Portuguese international Trade Routes can only target coastal cities or cities with a Harbor, but those routes gain a large yield bonus, and Portuguese Traders have increased range over water and can embark as soon as Trade Routes are unlocked, rewarding an ocean-spanning trade network.

**Unique Units**
- Nau (replaces Caravel) — A naval unit with a free promotion and lower maintenance than the Caravel, and it carries charges to build the unique Feitoria improvement, linking exploration directly to Portugal's trade economy.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Feitoria (Improvement) — Built only by a Nau on a foreign coastal city's tile next to a resource; Trade Routes sent to that city grant Portugal bonus Gold and Production, a unique way to profit from other civilizations' territory.
- Navigation School (Building) — Replaces the University and boosts Production toward naval units in its city, supporting Portugal's seafaring, exploration-heavy strategy.

### Historical Background
João III (reigned 1521-1557) ruled Portugal during the height of its overseas empire, consolidating and expanding the trading-post network established by his predecessors across the Atlantic, coastal Africa, India, and Southeast Asia through institutions like the Casa da Índia, which regulated the lucrative spice and goods trade with Portuguese Asia. His reign saw continued Portuguese exploration and colonization, including the beginnings of Portuguese Brazil's development, even as the crown increasingly relied on trade-post profits rather than large-scale territorial conquest. This coastal, trading-post-based model of empire—profit from controlled maritime trade routes rather than land conquest—is the direct basis for Portugal's Casa da Índia civilization ability and its unique Feitoria trading-post improvement.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Monitor
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Conditional | **Condition cost:** Water map plus coastal foreign cities that must actually exist and stay reachable

> The core loop is a stat check, not a build order. These guides fail hardest when written as sequences, because the player's real job is watching one number and responding to it.

### Setup
*Turns: 1 to roughly 25*

Establish the thing being monitored and the infrastructure that lets you respond to it.

- Name the watched stat on turn 1: Status of each Feitoria-hosting foreign city (still coastal, still standing, still reachable)
- Establish its bands before it matters, so the player recognizes trouble the first time rather than the third.
- Build the response capacity. A monitor with no available response is just anxiety.

### Standing Loop
*Turns: 25 to end of game*

This replaces the turn list entirely. The guide is this loop plus the era checkpoints.

- Read Status of each Feitoria-hosting foreign city (still coastal, still standing, still reachable).
- Green band: continue current build. No action.
- Amber band: begin the correction now. Amber is the acting band; red is the failing band.
- Red band: the ability is off or about to be. Everything else is deprioritized until it clears.

### Era Checkpoints
*Turns: each era boundary*

Re-read the role. Monitor leaders change what the stat means across eras.

- Ancient/Classical: Exploration and first contact with coastal civs.
- Medieval/Renaissance: Feitorias and high-yield foreign routes.
- Industrial/Modern: Strong trade economy, still externally dependent.
- Atomic/Information: Solid if the route network survived.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Watched stat sits in the red band for more than roughly 15 turns with no recovery path.
- Stop paying the Water map plus coastal foreign cities that must actually exist and stay reachable. If the bonus is unreachable, its price is pure loss.
- Rebuild as a generic civ around whatever the monitored strategy already produced.

### Fail state
The stat crossed into red and stayed. For Joao III, Routes require coastal foreign cities that must exist, be reachable, and remain in friendly hands.

### Caveats
- Routes require coastal foreign cities that must exist, be reachable, and remain in friendly hands.
- A rival conquering your route destinations damages your economy without touching your territory.
- Water-poor maps make the extra route capacity nearly useless.

### Guide must account for
- Exploration as a core early loop rather than a preliminary step.
- A target city audit maintained over time, since destinations change hands.
- Route re-planning triggers when destinations are lost or become hostile.
