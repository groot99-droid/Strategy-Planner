# Lady Six Sky — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Mayan  |  **Leader Title:** Queen of Naranjo
**Source:** New Frontier Pass

**Leader Ability — Ix Mutal Ajaw**
Within 6 tiles of her Capital, all of Lady Six Sky's units get bonus Combat Strength and her cities get a yield bonus, but any of her cities founded more than 6 tiles from the Capital instead take a yield penalty, strongly rewarding a small, tightly-clustered empire over a sprawling one.

**Agenda — Solitary**
Lady Six Sky wants to keep her own cities clustered close together near her Capital; she likes leaders who settle far away from her and dislikes those who encroach on her borders.

**Civilization Ability — Mayab**
Mayan cities don't get the normal Housing bonus from being adjacent to fresh water or Coast, but their Farms grant bonus Housing and Gold instead (with an added Production bonus next to an Observatory), letting the Maya build tall, food-driven cities away from rivers.

**Unique Units**
- Hul'che (replaces Archer) — An Ancient-era ranged unit with higher Ranged Strength than the Archer and a bonus against already-wounded units, giving the Maya strong early skirmishing power, especially near the Capital where Lady Six Sky's ability adds further Combat Strength.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Observatory (District) — Replaces the Campus; gets a major Science bonus from each adjacent Plantation and a minor bonus from adjacent Farms or districts, tying neatly into the Maya's farm-focused civilization ability.

### Historical Background
Lady Six Sky was a Maya queen who arrived at the city-state of Naranjo (in present-day Guatemala) around 682 CE and is credited with reviving it after a period of military defeat and decline, quite possibly having come from the powerful city of Dos Pilas or Calakmul. She oversaw military campaigns that restored Naranjo's regional power and, notably, placed her young son on the throne as king while continuing to wield significant authority herself, an unusual degree of female political power commemorated on Naranjo's carved stelae. That story of rebuilding and defending a single powerful city-state rather than pursuing distant conquest is mirrored directly in her in-game ability, which rewards a compact empire clustered tightly around the capital and penalizes overextension.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Front-Load
**Curve:** Flat | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Hard 6-tile expansion ceiling; penalties outside it

> Nearly every decision that matters is made in the first twenty turns and cannot be reversed. Guide density should be inverted: dense at the start, sparse after.

### Turn 0 — Assessment
*Turns: before the first Settler moves*

A genuine go or no-go on the start position. This phase exists only in this shape and it is the most valuable thing in the template.

- Check the hard requirement: Hard 6-tile expansion ceiling; penalties outside it
- If the requirement is absent within a reasonable walk, decide now whether to move the Settler or play the leader as a generic civ. Deciding this at turn 40 is deciding it too late.
- Estimate the ceiling. For radius- and continent-capped leaders, the ceiling is knowable early and determines the victory path.

### Turns 1-20 — Commit
*Turns: 1 to 20*

Make every irreversible decision deliberately rather than incidentally.

- Draw the full settle map before the second Settler is produced, including sites you will not reach for 40 turns.
- Assign district roles to each city site at settle time, not at build time. For placement-restricted leaders this is the whole game.
- Mark each decision with its reversibility. The guide should say plainly which choices are permanent.
- A settle map drawn before the first Settler moves, with every city site pre-assigned.

### Turns 20-80 — Execute
*Turns: 20 to 80*

Build out the plan drawn in the commit phase. Low decision density by design.

- Follow the settle map. Deviation is the common failure, and it usually feels reasonable at the time.
- Efficient compact core outperforming its size.

### Turn 80+ — Live With It
*Turns: 80 onward*

Operate inside the ceiling set at turn 20.

- Choose a victory path that fits the ceiling rather than fighting it.
- Hard ceiling reached. Efficiency without scale.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Turn 0 assessment fails and no acceptable alternative start is within reach.
- Play the leader as a generic civ and say so explicitly. A named fallback is better than a strategy that quietly cannot work.
- Target a faster victory, since the capped ceiling will not win a long game.

### Fail state
A placement decision made before turn 20 has capped the game, and the cap will not be visible until roughly turn 100. The city count ceiling is real and low. Domination is structurally awkward.

### Caveats
- The city count ceiling is real and low. Domination is structurally awkward.
- Captured cities outside the radius are penalized liabilities, not gains.
- The radius is measured from the capital, so a poor capital position is unrecoverable.

### Guide must account for
- A settle map drawn before the first Settler moves, with every city site pre-assigned.
- An explicit rule for conquered cities outside the radius: raze, gift, or accept the penalty.
- A victory path chosen early, since domination fights the kit.
