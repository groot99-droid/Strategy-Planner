# Eleanor of Aquitaine — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** France or England (player's choice at game start)  |  **Leader Title:** Queen of France and England
**Source:** Gathering Storm

**Leader Ability — Court of Love**
Every Great Work present in one of Eleanor's cities exerts a cumulative -1 Loyalty per turn on foreign cities within 9 tiles; if a foreign city's Loyalty is driven to 0 while Eleanor is exerting the most pressure on it, that city peacefully defects and joins her civilization — enabling a non-military, culture-driven form of conquest.

**Agenda — Angevin Empire**
Eleanor focuses on growing the Population of her own cities and dislikes leaders whose nearby cities have low Population, since low-population neighboring cities are easy prey for her Loyalty-pressure playstyle.

**Civilization Ability — Uses the chosen civilization's ability**
Eleanor is unique among Civ VI leaders in that the player chooses whether she leads France (gaining Grand Tour and France's unique units/infrastructure) or England (gaining England's civilization ability, currently 'Workshop of the World,' and units like the Sea Dog and Redcoat), while always keeping her own Court of Love leader ability and Angevin Empire agenda.

**Unique Units**
- Garde Impériale (if France) (replaces Line Infantry) — Available if Eleanor is played as France; strong on the home continent with Great General points from kills.
- Sea Dog (if England) (replaces Privateer) — Available if Eleanor is played as England; a strong naval raiding unit.
- Redcoat (if England) (replaces none (standalone Industrial era unit)) — Available if Eleanor is played as England; one of the strongest melee units through several eras, especially off the home continent.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Château (if France) (Improvement) — River-adjacent Culture improvement, bonus near Wonders.
- Royal Navy Dockyard (if England) (District) — England's Harbor replacement, boosting naval Production and strategic resource stockpiles.

### Historical Background
Eleanor of Aquitaine (c. 1122-1204) was one of medieval Europe's most powerful women, first as Queen of France through her marriage to Louis VII and later, after that marriage was annulled, as Queen of England through her marriage to Henry II — the basis for Civ VI's unusual choice to let players pick either civilization for her. She ruled vast territories in her own right as Duchess of Aquitaine, presided over a famously sophisticated court associated with the culture of courtly love (the namesake of her 'Court of Love' ability), and later backed her sons' rebellions against Henry II, spending years imprisoned as a result. Her personal patronage of troubadours, poets, and the arts across both French and English courts underlies her Great Works/Loyalty-pressure-based path to peacefully absorbing rival cities.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Dead-Phase
**Curve:** Bloom | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Needs a large Great Work stockpile before anything happens

> The hardest template to write and the most necessary. The leader contributes nothing for 80 to 120 turns. Without an explicit early game AND an explicit statement that the flatness is intended, players abandon the plan around turn 60.

### Phase A — Dormant
*Turns: 1 to roughly 130*

Survive, develop, and quietly accumulate the prerequisite the payoff phase consumes.

- Run a complete generic build order. The guide must supply this in full, because the leader supplies no direction.
- Maintain a defensive floor. Elimination during Phase A is the single most common way these leaders lose, and it is invisible in the ability description.
- Accumulate the prerequisite even though it produces nothing yet: A concentrated Great Work stockpile in border cities specifically, not spread empire-wide
- Provide non-ability progress markers so the player has feedback: city count, science rate, district count. Something must be visibly improving.

### Phase B — Activation
*Turns: roughly 130*

The kit switches on. Verify the accumulated prerequisite is actually sufficient.

- Check the stockpile. Activation with an empty stockpile wastes the entire dormant phase.
- Peak. Neighboring cities defecting.
- Reorient production toward the payoff. Phase A build habits are now wrong.

### Phase C — Payoff
*Turns: 130 onward*

Convert the accumulated position at speed. The remaining game is short, so this phase runs hot.

- Consolidated empire acquired without war.
- Push the victory condition directly. There is no time for a second build-out.

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Reaching activation with the prerequisite stockpile empty, or having lost cities during the dormant phase.
- Accept a lesser victory path built on the generic empire rather than the leader ability.
- The leader ability is now decorative. Say so and stop planning around it.

### Fail state
Crippled or eliminated before activation, or arriving at activation with nothing stockpiled. Both are Phase A failures that only become visible in Phase B.

### Caveats
- The strategy requires neighbors close enough for loyalty pressure to reach, which is a map condition.
- Great Works must be concentrated in border cities, not distributed for safety.
- Flipped cities can flip back if the pressure balance shifts.

### Guide must account for
- Border city selection made early and deliberately, since the whole plan concentrates there.
- Great Work placement as a strategic decision rather than a storage question.
- Loyalty pressure math surfaced to the player, because the mechanism is otherwise invisible.
