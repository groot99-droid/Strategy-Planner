# Shaka — Strategic Planning Guide

## Civilization VI Profile

**Civilization:** Zulu  |  **Leader Title:** King of the Zulu
**Source:** Rise and Fall

**Leader Ability — Amabutho**
Allows the Zulus to form military units into Corps and Armies far earlier than other civilizations, unlocking these formations ahead of the normal Mercenaries and Nationalism civic requirements.

**Agenda — Horn, Chest, Loins**
Shaka tries to organize as many of his units as possible into Corps and Armies, and dislikes civilizations that keep their military as unconsolidated single units.

**Civilization Ability — Isibongo**
Garrisoned units boost the Loyalty of their cities, and units automatically upgrade into Corps, Armies, Fleets, or Armadas upon capturing a city once the relevant civics are known, making Zulu conquest self-reinforcing.

**Unique Units**
- Impi (replaces Pikeman) — A cheap, low-maintenance Pikeman upgrade with a strong flanking bonus and faster experience gain, ideal for forming into Corps and Armies early.

**Unique Infrastructure**
- Ikanda (District) — Built in half the normal time, letting the Zulu establish military infrastructure and start training Impi far earlier than rival civilizations.

### Historical Background
Shaka was king of the Zulu Kingdom in the early 19th century and one of the most transformative military leaders in southern African history, credited with reorganizing Zulu warfare around the short stabbing spear (iklwa), the 'buffalo horns' encirclement tactic, and age-grade regiments housed in fortified military kraals called amakhanda (singular ikanda) — the direct namesake of the civilization's unique district. His reforms fueled the Zulu Kingdom's rapid expansion and contributed to the wider upheaval and displacement across the region known as the Mfecane. His emphasis on organized, disciplined regiments is mirrored in-game by his leader ability's early access to Corps and Armies.

## Leadership Focus Guide

**Shape:** Gate
**Curve:** Ramp | **Conversion:** Permanent | **Condition cost:** Genuinely weak until Mercenaries

> The strategy does not begin on a turn. It begins on an unlock. This needs two complete guides with a trigger between them, not one guide with a note in the middle.

### Pre-Gate
*Turns: 1 until the gate opens*

Survive, develop, and stage. This is a real phase with its own complete build order, not a waiting room.

- State the gate explicitly on turn 1 so the player knows what they are working toward: Mercenaries civic unlocked
- Give an estimated arrival turn AND the research or civic path that gets there fastest.
- Build the prerequisites the gate assumes but does not provide. Genuinely weak until Mercenaries
- Maintain a defensive floor. The gate does not protect you while you wait for it.

### Gate Check
*Turns: variable*

A single decision point, not a phase.


### Post-Gate
*Turns: gate onward*

The actual leader strategy, now live.

- Peak. Corps snowball with auto-upgrade on capture.
- Run the closing-window check: most Gate abilities are also time-limited on the far end. Identify when parity arrives.
- Convert before parity. Permanent

### Abort branch
**Trigger:** Gate opens with staging incomplete and a rival is already at parity.
- Do not open the strategy. Playing an unstaged Gate leader into a prepared opponent is the worst outcome available.
- Fold into a generic plan built on whatever the pre-gate phase produced.

### Fail state
The gate opened, the window closed, and the strategy never ran. The pre-gate phase was treated as filler and produced nothing usable.

### Caveats
- The early game is a real vulnerability, not a slow start. Getting rushed ends the game before the kit exists.
- The +5 edge is an edge over civs without Corps. It shrinks to nothing once everyone has them.
- The go signal is a civic unlock, not a turn number, and it varies by game.

### Guide must account for
- A defensive phase with its own complete build order, treated as a real phase and not filler.
- A gate condition (Mercenaries) that triggers the transition, replacing any fixed turn.
- A closing window warning: the snowball must be underway before rivals reach Corps parity.
